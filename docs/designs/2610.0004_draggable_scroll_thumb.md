# 2610.0004 Draggable scroll thumb

> **Doc status: HISTORICAL** — a design record for work that has already
> shipped. It describes the system *as of the change it accompanied*, not as it
> is today. For current behaviour see [`../architecture.md`](../architecture.md);

**Date:** 2026-10-04
**Status:** implemented
**Author:** EP Lin
**Doc type:** Mini RFC
**Issue:** [#193](https://github.com/pingeplin/tarmac/issues/193)
**Builds on:** [2610.0003 Card scroll indicator](2610.0003_card_scroll_indicator.md) (#191, PR #192) — and reverses its second confirmed decision, "Passive"

## Decision

A press on the scroll thumb, while it shows, drags it, and the card's content
follows: a terminal's viewport, a markdown doc's scroller, an HTML document's
root.

Three things were chosen by the user before this was written (2026-10-04):

- **When it can be grabbed.** The thumb still appears only when the selected
  card takes a wheel. While the pointer is over it, or holds it, it does not
  fade.
- **The edge.** Where the thumb is shown it takes the press ahead of the
  card's resize strip, which keeps the rest of the edge.
- A separate change, after #192.

One thing this design chooses: **during a drag the thumb is still drawn from
what the content reports, not at the pointer.** The pointer says where the
content should go; the content goes there and says where it is; the thumb is
drawn from that, as it is for a wheel. We value a thumb that never says
anything the content has not said over a thumb glued to the cursor, given
three engines that each answer at their own pace.

## Problem

2610.0003 made the thumb look-only and hid WebKit's scrollbars in the two web
kinds, so nothing on a card can be dragged to scroll. The wheel is the only
way through long content, and on this board's own QA cards that is:

| Card | Content | Screens of wheel, end to end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A terminal with 1845 rows of scrollback, 15 showing | 1860 rows | 123 |
| `qa/scroll-indicator-long.md` | 11484 px in a 278 px body | 40 |
| `qa/wheel-travel-probe.html` | 48720 units in 840 | 57 |

The thumb is now 10 wide and looks like something to grab. On first seeing it
in the app the user asked to move it with the cursor.

## Constraints

- **Three engines, three ways in.** A terminal's viewport is libghostty's:
  the pinned header has an absolute-row scroll
  (`GHOSTTY_SCROLL_VIEWPORT_ROW`, in the row space of the scrollbar's
  `offset`), which `TerminalEngine` does not expose yet. A markdown page is
  the app's own, scriptable in the card world. An HTML card's document is in a
  sandboxed iframe with an opaque origin: the app reaches it only by a message
  from the host page, and the shim takes two today, `zoom` and `cull`.
- **The thumb is chrome**, a sibling of the body's container in the card's
  clip. Its frame is rewritten at every report, so it moves under a pointer
  that is dragging it.
- **`CardView.hitTest` hands every point in a resize zone to the grip**,
  whatever is under it. The right strip is the card's outer 6 screen points
  and the corners 20 × 20: at zoom 1 the strip covers 3 of the thumb's 10
  points, and at about zoom 0.4 and below all of it.
- **The wheel router gives a card the wheel only when the view under the
  pointer is inside the body's container.** The thumb is not.
- **After its fade the thumb's view is at alpha 0, not hidden**, and AppKit's
  hit test does not look at alpha.
- **`shown` in the dev snapshot is `Visibility`'s word**, and spec 2610.0003's
  live scenarios assert its timing.
- **`TarmacApp` has no unit tests.** A decision goes in `TarmacKit`; the shell
  reads state, calls it and applies the answer.

## Proposed change

### The rule: two more things hold the thumb

`ScrollIndicator.Visibility` is told two more things, and stays the one place
that says whether the thumb shows:

```swift
extension ScrollIndicator.Visibility {
    /// Told at every pointer move, after every layout of the thumb, at a
    /// wheel and at the release.
    public mutating func pointer(over: Bool, atMs: UInt64)
    /// The hit rule let the press through; it is taken.
    public mutating func pressed(atMs: UInt64)
    /// Nothing with no press held: a release that follows `reset()`.
    public mutating func released(atMs: UInt64)
    /// alpha > 0: a fading thumb can still be caught.
    public func grabbable(atMs: UInt64) -> Bool
    /// When the fade starts; nil while the thumb is pressed or hovered, or
    /// not showing.
    public func fadeStartsAtMs(atMs: UInt64) -> UInt64?
}
```

- A wheel shows the thumb, as today. **Hover alone shows nothing**: a pointer
  that arrives over a thumb that has faded leaves it faded.
- While the pointer is over a showing thumb, or a press holds it, `alpha` is 1.
  Showing is `alpha` above 0: a pointer that reaches the thumb inside its fade
  brings it back to 1, as a press there does.
- When the last of the two ends, the one-second hold starts again from that
  moment, then the fade.
- A pointer already resting where the thumb appears counts as over it: the
  thumb then stays until the pointer moves off.
- `reset()` — the card was deselected — drops all of it, a press included. The
  drag held with that press ends too (*The drag*, below).

The card's one timer is armed for `fadeStartsAtMs` after every change, instead
of the shell working the instant out from the two durations as it does today.

Whether the pointer is over the thumb is one question — does the window's hit
test, at the pointer's position, answer this card's thumb. It is the hit test
the cursor router already runs, and it goes through the card's (*Who takes
the press*, below), so a card or an overlay lying over the thumb is not the
thumb. It is asked at four moments, each of which calls
`pointer(over:atMs:)`:

- **a pointer move.** `HoverCursorRouter` already hit-tests every move to set
  the cursor; it now also says which thumb, if any, the pointer is over, and
  that it has left the one it was over — the board included, when the pointer
  leaves it. No tracking area is added.
- **after each layout of the thumb**, because the thumb moves under a still
  pointer.
- **at a wheel**, after it is recorded: a pointer resting where the thumb
  appears is then over it, also after a deselect dropped what was known.
- **at the release**: the router sees no move during a drag, and a drag that
  ends clamped at the track's end brings no report and so no layout.

A faded thumb answers no hit, so a pointer over it is not over it: that is
"hover alone shows nothing".

### Who takes the press

A pure rule in `TarmacKit` says what a point on a card is, in this order:

1. **the thumb** — the point is in the thumb's frame and
   `Visibility.grabbable`;
2. **a resize handle** — `CardHandles.handle(at:)`, as today;
3. **the content** — whatever AppKit finds.

`CardView.hitTest` asks it and returns the thumb view, the grip or the hit.
`ScrollThumbView.hitTest` itself keeps answering nil, so a thumb that is not
grabbable is not there at all: no press lands on a faded thumb, and the
resize strip has its whole edge back. The rule is the one gate: a press it
let through is taken, with no second look at the clock.

The view that takes a `mouseDown` gets the drag and the release, as the grip
does (as built, the release is read off the application's events: a hidden
thumb is sent none). The press monitor still runs first and selects the card, which it
already is: a thumb shows only on the selected card.

A press on the thumb reaches nothing under it: no click in a document, no
double-click that borrows an HTML card, no selection or mouse report in a
terminal. The thumb takes no keyboard focus, so a borrowed card stays
borrowed and a terminal keeps the keys. Over the thumb the cursor is the
arrow — not the resize cursor in the overlap, and not WebKit's I-beam (the
thumb claims pointer moves over a web body, as the grip does).

### A wheel over the thumb still scrolls

Once the thumb answers a hit, a wheel over it would pan the board — the
router does not find it in the body — and would reach no content, since
nothing in the thumb's responder chain scrolls. Both are put right.
`BoardWheel.route`, the router's tested rule, is told the pointer is over the
thumb and gives the card the wheel for it as it does for the body — all of
the thumb: while it shows, a wheel on the part the resize strip covers
scrolls the card, where 2610.0003 had it pan the board. And the thumb passes
a wheel to the view under it in the body, as the HTML card's shield does —
found by asking the body's container, since the card's own hit test now
answers the thumb. 2610.0003's S39 (a wheel over the thumb scrolls the card
and the board does not move) stands; its "takes no pointer" becomes "takes
the press, not the wheel".

### The drag

A pure value in `TarmacKit`, beside `ScrollIndicator`, in the manner of
`CardDrag`:

- **At the press** it keeps one number: how far below the thumb's top the
  pointer is.
- **At each drag event** it is given the pointer and the track as it is laid
  out *then* — the same body, cover, scale and metrics `frame` takes — and
  answers the offset that would put the thumb's top that far above the
  pointer: the inverse of `ScrollIndicator.thumb`, clamped to
  `0 … total − visible`.

It never reads where the thumb is drawn after the press, so the thumb
trailing the pointer cannot feed back into where the content is sent. A press
alone scrolls nothing; the first drag event does. Pointer positions are in
the card layer's coordinates, which hold still while the thumb's frame is
rewritten.

The card holds one such value, nil between presses, as `CardGestureSlot`
holds a move. It is held from the press to the release, and ends the same way
when it is cut short: the card is deselected, removed, or leaves its window
with the pointer down. A drag event with no drag held answers nothing. A drag
event with no thumb laid out — the metrics are gone after a reload, or the
content now fits — answers nothing too, and the drag stays held: it picks up
from the pointer once a thumb is laid out again. (As built: a hidden thumb is
sent no event at all, a release included, so the card reads the release off
the application's events — spec 2610.0004, S29.)

The offset is in the metrics' own unit, which is each content's own: rows,
the scroller's pixels, the document's units. So nothing converts it.

### Scrolling each kind to an offset

| Kind | New | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Terminal | `TerminalView.scroll(to:)`, over a new `ViewportScroll.row` | rounds the offset to the nearest row (`TarmacTermTests`), then the engine's absolute-row scroll and the frame read a wheel schedules — so it redraws and reports |
| Markdown | `tarmacDoc.scrollTo(y)` | sets the scroller's `scrollTop`; its `scroll` listener reports, as for a wheel |
| HTML | a host message `{"tarmac": "scrollTo", "y": n}` | the shim scrolls the root to `y` at once — `behavior: "instant"`, whatever the document's `scroll-behavior` — keeping `scrollX`; the document's `scroll` event reports |

`DocCardBody` gains `scroll(to:)`, which both web bodies implement. The card
does not know a terminal view's type, so it hands the offset to
`AppController` through a callback, the way a terminal's metrics come to it.

**The message to an HTML document.** #144 removed the app's only
host-to-document scroll message, a `scrollBy` relayed per wheel event, because
its travel was wrong and it could not reach what was under the pointer. This
one is not that: it is an absolute position of the root — exactly what the
thumb stands for — sent only while the user holds the thumb. The shim honours
it, like `zoom` and `cull`, only from its parent window, and only for a finite
number; a document cannot send it to itself, and it gives the host nothing a
wheel does not already do. It schedules nothing, so spec 2609.0002's cull
gate stands. 2610.0002's S23 stands too — a host message named `scroll`
scrolls nothing: this one is `scrollTo`, and the shim still knows no `scroll`.

A terminal program that tracks the mouse gets no report for a press on the
thumb: while the thumb shows, that press is Tarmac's.

**The terminal's live edge**, measured on a 3-row engine holding ten lines
(2026-10-04): the absolute-row scroll to `total − visible`, or to any row past
it, leaves `isViewportAtBottom` true, and the viewport follows the next
output; scrolled to an earlier row, output grows `total` and leaves `offset`
where it is; on the alternate screen it does nothing. So a thumb dragged to
the bottom leaves a terminal that follows its output again, with no rule of
its own for the end of the range.

### The driver sees it

`cards[].scroll` gains `thumb`, the thumb's rect in the coordinates of
`screen_rect` (or `null` when none is laid out), built by `DevSnapshot`. A
tool outside the app can then aim at the thumb instead of reading it off a
capture. `shown` keeps its meaning.

`scripts/qa/wheel-gesture.swift` learns to move the pointer with no wheel, to
press, drag in steps and release, and to leave the pointer where it is.

## Why this and not the alternatives

**Draw the thumb at the pointer while it is held.** It would feel glued. But
then the thumb says something the content has not: when the content cannot
follow — a document that pins its root, a total that changed mid-drag — the
thumb is wrong until release, and something has to reconcile the two. If the
live check of the lag (below) fails, this is the fallback. It changes where
the thumb is drawn while pressed, not where the content is sent; what it adds
is listed under *Riskiest assumption*.

**Give the web kinds WebKit's scrollbar back** and build only the terminal's.
Dragging would be free there. 2610.0003 already turned this down: three
looks, and a magnified HTML card's scrollbar is about 1 pt wide at zoom 0.25.

**Move the content by posting wheel events.** No new message, no new engine
call. But a wheel is relative: it cannot land on a position, it rounds
differently in each kind, and it brings momentum.

**Let `ScrollThumbView` answer its own hit test.** Less code. But an alpha-0
thumb would take presses nobody can see, and "the thumb beats the strip while
it shows" would be untested shell.

**Show the thumb on hover at the edge, or always on the selected card; keep
the thumb clear of the resize strip.** Offered to the user, not taken.

## Trade-offs accepted

- **The thumb trails the pointer** during a drag by the time the content
  takes to report: a frame read for a terminal, a message each way for a web
  card. A terminal's thumb also moves a whole row at a time — with 15 rows of
  history, on a card of the size one opens at (15 rows showing, a track of
  286 pt), that is 9.5 pt of thumb a row.
- **Where the thumb shows, the edge under it does not resize.** At zoom 1
  that is half the strip's width along the thumb's length; at about zoom 0.4
  and below, all of it. At the track's end the thumb also reaches into the
  bottom-right corner zone.
- **A host-to-document scroll message is back**, absolute this time.
- **A program tracking the mouse** in a terminal misses presses in the
  thumb's column while the thumb shows. So does a borrowed HTML document, and
  a markdown page's text.
- **A thumb under a resting pointer stays.** It is opaque, so the content
  under its 10 units is not read until the pointer moves off.
- **Pointer-over is read only while the window is key**: the board's pointer
  moves stop when it is not, and a wheel still reaches it. A thumb shown by a
  wheel in a window that is not key, under a resting pointer, holds until the
  window is key and the pointer moves.
- **Only an HTML document's root** is dragged, as only it is indicated. A
  document that will not scroll leaves the thumb where it was.
- **More untested shell**: mouse handlers, the router's word on the thumb, a
  wheel hand-over, and `tarmacDoc.scrollTo` (no test runs `doc-render.js`).
  Each is covered by a live scenario with a knockout.

## Riskiest assumption

That a thumb drawn from reports follows the pointer closely enough to feel
dragged. The parts are known one by one — a terminal reports at its next
frame read, a web card after a `scroll` event and a message — but nobody has
watched them under a held pointer, and 2610.0003 left the web kinds' latency
unmeasured ("a frame or two").

It is measured as soon as a drag can be posted — once Plan step 3's wiring is
in, before its live scenarios — in two ways. A stepped drag: the snapshot's
`thumb` is read 50 ms after each step, three frames at 60 Hz. And a run:
drag events 16 ms apart for a second, in case scroll-to calls queue, with the
`thumb` read 50 ms after the last. Both are run on each of the three kinds.
If either, on any kind, reads a thumb whose top is not where the pointer
holds it (to a device pixel plus one unit of the content), the thumb is drawn
at the pointer while pressed instead, on all three — one rule, as the thumb
is one; the mapping, the three scroll-to paths and the hit rule stay as they
are.

The fallback adds three things. The held thumb's frame comes from the
pointer, clamped to the track: a coordinate, so a `TarmacKit` function. A
report's frame is held back while pressed and applied at the release, which
is the reconcile. And the snapshot's `thumb` then says where the pointer is,
so criterion 3 measures nothing and goes.

Two more facts the mechanisms rest on have no measurement recorded here.
`window.scrollTo` in a magnified root lands at the `scrollY` it was given:
2610.0003 measured the reads, not a write, and criterion 2 is its check. And
`behavior: "instant"` beats a document's `scroll-behavior: smooth`: that is
the CSSOM's word, and where it fails only that document's thumb trails.

## Success criteria

1. **The rules, the mapping and the message** (unit tests): `Visibility`
   holds `alpha` at 1 while the pointer is over a showing thumb or a press
   holds it, starts the hold again when the last of the two ends, shows
   nothing for a pointer alone, and drops all of it at `reset`. The drag's
   offset is the inverse of `ScrollIndicator.thumb` — an offset mapped to a
   thumb and back is itself, to one unit — and is clamped at both ends; a
   press with no drag yields no scroll. The hit rule answers the thumb ahead
   of a handle only while it is grabbable, and `BoardWheel.route` gives the
   card a wheel over the thumb. A terminal's absolute-row scroll does what
   was measured above, and `scroll(to:)` rounds to the nearest row
   (`TarmacTermTests`). The shim scrolls the root, with `behavior: "instant"`,
   for a `scrollTo` with a finite `y` from its parent and for nothing else,
   and asks for no native frame and no timer for it, paused or not
   (`CardShimTests`, as 2610.0003's S24); its test that a host message named
   `scroll` scrolls nothing stays as it is.
2. **Each kind follows**: on a selected, overflowing terminal, markdown card
   and HTML card, dragging the thumb to the track's end gives
   `offset + visible = total` (± 1 for the web kinds), back to the top gives
   0, and a drag of half the free track gives half of `total − visible` to
   1 % or one unit, whichever is larger.
3. **The lag**, on each of the three kinds: a snapshot asked for 50 ms after
   each step of a stepped drag, and 50 ms after the last of a run of drag
   events 16 ms apart for a second, reads the `thumb` top at the pointer less
   the grab, to one device pixel plus one unit of the content — that unit as
   the thumb travels for it, `(track − length) / (total − visible)`. Both
   drags stay inside the free track: past its end the clamp puts the thumb at
   the pointer, not the content. The snapshot is a CLI call, so a reading is
   later than 50 ms by the call's own time; the script times each call and
   the sheet records it.
4. **Hover holds**: with the pointer left on a showing thumb `shown` is still
   true after 3 s; once it is moved off, `shown` is false within 1.5 s. A
   pointer brought onto a faded thumb leaves `shown` false.
5. **The edge**: at zoom 1, with the thumb at the top of its track, a press
   on its outer 3 points, dragged 50 pt down and 50 pt right, changes
   `offset` and not the card's `board_rect`; the same press and drag with the
   thumb faded leaves `offset` and makes `board_rect` 50 wider. (The right
   strip resizes on a sideways drag only: dragged straight down, the faded
   case changes nothing.)
6. **Nothing under it is pressed**: after a thumb drag on the probe while it
   is borrowed, the probe's `mousedown` count is unchanged and `borrowed` is
   still true (unborrowed, the shield stops every press and the count proves
   nothing); two quick presses on an unborrowed HTML card's thumb leave
   `borrowed` false; a press on the thumb of a terminal whose program tracks
   the mouse and prints what it is sent leaves its `scrollback_tail` as it
   was.
7. **A wheel over the thumb scrolls** the card and the board does not move:
   2610.0003's S39 reads as recorded — `offset` grows by 30 ± 1, and
   `viewport.cx` and `viewport.cy` are what they were.
8. `make test` and `make qa` pass, and 2610.0003's live scenarios whose code
   this change touches still read as recorded: S39 with the pointer on the
   thumb; S19, S28, S29, S30, S31, S36 and S37 with it off. (S16–S18 and S35
   check the three reports and the hidden scrollbars, whose code it does not
   edit.) Of its unit scenarios one changes: S15's pinned `scroll` object
   gains `thumb`.

## Out of scope

A press on the track that pages; a horizontal thumb; a thumb for a nested
scroller; keys; a thumb on a card that is not selected; a `tarmac dev` verb
that drags (the gesture script is the tool).

## Plan

1. **Kit and engines**: `Visibility`'s new inputs, the drag mapping, the hit
   rule, `BoardWheel.route`'s thumb, `ViewportScroll.row` and
   `TerminalView.scroll(to:)`, `tarmacDoc.scrollTo`, the shim's `scrollTo`
   and `CardHostMessage`, `DevSnapshot`'s `thumb`. Test-first.
2. **The gesture script**: move, press, drag, dwell.
3. **Wiring, then the lag check** (criterion 3) before the rest of the live
   scenarios: `CardView.hitTest`, the thumb's mouse handlers and cursor, the
   cursor router's word on the thumb, the wheel hand-over, the wheel router.
4. **QA sheet and docs**: `docs/architecture.md`, the dev-channel skill; one
   line on 2610.0003's design saying its "Passive" is superseded here. Its
   spec and QA sheet stay as the record of #192.

## Confirmed (2026-10-04)

The user approved the design as written, and chose, where asked:

1. **The thumb follows the content** during a drag: drawn from reports, with
   the measured fallback — past 50 ms of lag on any one kind, it is drawn at
   the pointer while pressed, on all three.
2. **A press on the track away from the thumb does nothing new.**

Approved with it, as the draft assumed:

3. A pointer resting where the thumb appears counts as over it, so that thumb
   stays until the pointer moves off.
4. A scroll message from the host to an HTML document returns, absolute.
5. A press on the thumb in a terminal is never reported to a program
   tracking the mouse.
6. The cursor over the thumb is the arrow.
7. No drag threshold: the first drag event scrolls.
8. A drag whose thumb goes away under it — a reload, content that now fits —
   scrolls nothing and stays held until the release.
