# 2608.0002 Doc-card raster cost — de-promote, don't re-zoom

> **Doc status: HISTORICAL** — a design record for the change it accompanied.
> It describes the system *as of that change*, not as it is today.

**Date:** 2026-08-27 · spelling decision recorded 2026-08-28
**Status:** implemented (see Verification)
**Author:** EP Lin
**Doc type:** design record
**Issue:** [#76](https://github.com/pingeplin/tarmac/issues/76)
**Relates to:** [2608.0001](2608.0001_frozen_k_magnify_for_html_cards.md) (frozen-K magnify), [#75](https://github.com/pingeplin/tarmac/issues/75), [#94](https://github.com/pingeplin/tarmac/issues/94)

## TL;DR

Issue #76 is right that doc cards burn K²(=9×) raster memory at every zoom, and the real number is worse than it claims. Its proposed fix — CSS `zoom`
tracking board zoom — is **falsified**: that mechanism produces seven distinct document layouts across nine zoom steps and structurally breaks
`savedScrollFrac`, and the infrastructure it plans to reuse does not exist.

The memory goal is reachable in CSS alone, because the K× allocation is **promotion-driven, not height-driven**, and its sole surviving cause is
`.doc-prose pre { overflow: auto }`. De-promoting that subtree drops a 6-card board from **261 → 81 MB** (short docs) and **178 → 93 MB** (long docs),
prose wrap points byte-identical to today. The spelling is **decided** — `overflow: visible; white-space: pre-wrap; word-break: break-word`, three
declarations and no JS ([Design](#design), [Decisions](#decisions) §1). Issue #76's second goal, `MAX_ZOOM > 3`, is **not delivered**, and whether
de-promotion changes the crispness ceiling is **UNKNOWN**. Every claim is tagged **MEASURED**, **REASONED** or **UNKNOWN**.

## Problem

Three separate things, all part of #76's case, and they do not all hold.

### 1. The memory premise is CONFIRMED, and understated — MEASURED

Three instruments across two engine builds agreed within ~3%: WebKit rasterizes the transformed subtree at its **layout (K×) size** and down-scales at
composite time. `.doc-prose-scaler`'s compositedBounds for a 392×310 card is **invariant in board zoom** — 1176 × 13064 at K=3, 784 × 8900 at K=2,
392 × 4712 at K=1, identical at z = 0.5, 1, 2, 3 — and on one 600×1000 card (dpr 2, z = 1) the K² law reads 9.38 / 37.50 / 84.38 MB at K = 1 / 2 / 3. The
issue says the cost is paid "including 1× and below"; it is worse, because the effective penalty is **(K/z)²**, so zooming *out* costs relatively
more, and short documents are the worst case — an untiled layer allocates its whole compositedBounds, so the clean 9× lands, while a tall layer is
tile-capped at ~6×. Two side notes that cost time to learn: **`will-change` is irrelevant to memory** (byte-identical 18.38 MB either way;
`docZoom.ts:90`'s warning is about raster *pinning*), and **`ps -o rss=` is completely blind to this** (K=3 → 363 MB, K=1 → 390 MB, empty board
→ 364 MB — no signal, because composited backing stores are IOSurface-backed).

### 2. The proposed mechanism is FALSIFIED — MEASURED

Three probe arms over identical content and identical base metrics: **A** — #76 as issued, prose `zoom` = board zoom (changing); **B** — frozen `zoom:
K` + outer `scale(z/K)`; **C** — shipped control, metrics hand-multiplied by K. Arm A produced **seven distinct whole-document layouts across nine
zoom steps**; arms B and C were byte-stable at every step, on both engines, at both dpr, at both card widths (392 and 460). Line-start character
offsets for **one paragraph** (`p1`) — a single block's signature, not the whole-document fingerprint that yields the seven — identical on both
engines; count the *distinct rows*, and this block alone resolves to **five** signatures across nine steps.

| z | `p1` line-start offsets | lines |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | 0, 36, 74, 106, 138, 174, 207, 241 | 8 |
| 0.55 | 0, 36, 81, 121, 155, 191, 223, 265 | 8 |
| 0.75 | 0, 51, 95, 138, 184, 233 | 6 |
| 1 | 0, 51, 95, 138, 184, 233 | 6 |
| 1.37 | 0, 51, 106, 155, 207, 258 | 6 |
| 1.5 | 0, 58, 114, 165, 215, 265 | 6 |
| 2 / 2.5 / 3 | 0, 58, 114, 165, 215, 265 | 6 |

The line **count** holds at 6 from z = 0.75 to z = 3 while the breaks move through three distinct regimes — which is why character offsets are the
right instrument and a line count is not, the lesson 2608.0001 learned about `wrap-probe.html`. It also forecloses the obvious rescue: clamping
zoom ≥ 1 stabilises nothing, because the drift lives *above* 1 as well as below. Non-vacuity checks that passed: Arm A at z = 3 reproduces Arm B
byte-for-byte; fresh-load-at-z agrees with mutate-in-place, so layout is a pure function of z; dpr 1 and dpr 2 are identical, so this is a layout
effect, not a raster artifact.

**`savedScrollFrac` breaks structurally under Arm A** — MEASURED. Content height, normalised to z = 1:

| arm | z = 0.5 | z = 1 | z ≥ 1.5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| C (shipped) | 1098 | 1098 | 1098 |
| A (#76) | 1812 | 1152 | 1104 |

A stored fraction of 0.45 lands at own-unit 814 / 518 / 497 — a spread of ~317 units, roughly **13 lines**. It is a *consequence* of the reflow, not
an independent bug, so it cannot be fixed while keeping the mechanism.

### 3. The infrastructure #76 plans to reuse does not exist — MEASURED (code inventory)

All four of #76's infrastructure assumptions fail against the tree. The #75 capability probe is structurally **iframe-only**
(`desktop/src-tauri/src/card_shim.js:125-141` → `zoomCapable()` in `desktop/src/kit/zoomMode.ts:38-42` → only `desktop/src/cards/HtmlCard.tsx:112`),
and [#94](https://github.com/pingeplin/tarmac/issues/94) deletes it. `onRasterScaleSettle` (`desktop/src/board/BoardEngine.ts:91`) delivers a
*quantized rasterScale*, not a settled zoom — `deriveRasterScale` (`desktop/src/kit/rasterScale.ts:41-45`) steps by 0.5, so z 2.1 → 2.3 fires nothing
and z ≤ 1 never fires — and its single slot is already claimed by `TerminalCard` (`desktop/src/board/Board.tsx:82`). `DocCard.tsx` has no settle
concept at all (an undebounced `MutationObserver` on `.board`'s style attribute, `desktop/src/cards/DocCard.tsx:94-105`), so #76 would be
*introducing* settle to doc cards. And the "legacy-WebKit fallback" is void under the macOS 26 floor. Blast radius the issue omits:
`desktop/src/kit/termZoom.ts:8` re-exports `docWrapperBox`/`docCardVars` verbatim, so any `docZoom.ts` signature change lands on `TerminalCard` too.

## Root cause

**The K× allocation is not height-driven — it is promotion-driven.** MEASURED.

`.doc-prose-scaler` carries `scale(zoom/K)`, which promotes it to its own composited layer, and a composited layer is sized by its element's box
rather than by the region actually painted. So it is allocated at the full K× layout box — 1176 × 13064 for a 392×310 card — and stays that size at
every board zoom, the down-scale being applied *after* that allocation. That is the whole 9×. De-promote it and the K× prose paints directly into the
scroller's layer, already bounded to the real-px scrollport: the oversample survives as a rendering technique, and only the private backing store goes
away.

Promotion looked over-determined, but measuring each of the three plausible causes in isolation found **exactly one survivor**: `.doc-prose pre {
overflow: auto }` (`desktop/src/theme/card.css:302-307`), and only when a code block *actually* overflows horizontally (`scroll` still promotes;
`hidden`, `clip` and `visible` de-promote identically; `<img>`, `<iframe>` and non-overflowing `auto` divs never promote). So **a doc with no
horizontally-overflowing `<pre>` is already on the cheap path today** — MEASURED. The pathology is specific to wide code blocks, i.e. most
agent-authored markdown — **REASONED**, since with one document family as the evidence base, what fraction of a real corpus carries them is unknown.

## Design

Three declarations in `desktop/src/theme/card.css`, replacing `overflow: auto`:

```css
.doc-prose pre { overflow: visible; white-space: pre-wrap; word-break: break-word; }
```

Nothing else changes and no JS is added: `docZoom.ts` is untouched, `DOC_OVERSAMPLE_K` stays 3, `.doc-prose-sizer` and `relayout()` stay as they are,
`savedScrollFrac` keeps working, no protocol key moves, and `docZoom.test.ts` S12/S13 keep passing unchanged.

### A premise correction a spec must not inherit

**`scrollHeight` is not constant across zoom in the real app, in any arm** — MEASURED. It is `ceil(natH·z/K)` in the shipped build, under `clip`, and
under the chosen arm alike; the LONG corpus reads 2261 / 4521 / 9042 / 13563 at z = 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 3. The "3278 at every z" figure an earlier draft of
this doc leaned on belongs to `desktop/qa/doc-prose-zoom-probe.html` arm C, where **no JS sets the sizer height** — a probe artifact, not app
behaviour. A spec written against this doc must therefore assert **natH zoom-invariance, fingerprint stability and scroll-fraction round-trip** —
never a constant `scrollHeight`. Said explicitly because a spec will be written against this page next.

### Finding 8 — the chosen arm, end to end — MEASURED

**The canonical table.** One session, pinned once and held across every arm: dpr 2 (verified at the window on every Engine B row), board zoom 1,
K = 3, six 392×310 cards in a 1400×900 window, macOS 26.6.1; calibration rows reproduce the established baselines exactly. Arm `wrapx` is `overflow:
visible; white-space: pre-wrap`; arm `wrap` is `wrapx` plus `word-break: break-word`.

| arm | Engine B SHORT | Engine B LONG | Engine A SHORT | Engine A LONG |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| base (shipped `overflow: auto`) | 261 MB | 178 MB | 240.44 | 157.41 |
| `clip` | 76 | 93 | 59.69 | 75.39 |
| `wrapx` | 82 | 93 | 64.50 | 75.39 |
| **`wrap` (chosen)** | **81** | **93** | **64.50** | **75.39** |

- **The chosen arm costs +6 MB (+7.9%) against `clip` on SHORT and nothing on LONG.** The saving is *not* identical to `clip`'s and this doc no longer
  says it is. 100% of the SHORT surcharge is attributed to the doc scroller's own tiled backing store (`.doc-scroll`, 21.32 → 26.13 MB across six
  cards); every other layer is byte-identical. LONG is already past the tile cap, hence the zero delta.
- **De-promotion is genuine, not partial.** `scalerN = 0` and the layer is absent from the tree at every step of a 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 1 sweep.
  Adversarial check: a `<pre>` that really overflows under `visible` (scrollWidth 4693 vs clientWidth 1044) still yields zero scalers.
- **No ratchet.** Engine A returns to exactly 64.50 / 75.39 after each of 24 full-range scroll cycles plus settles. Engine B drifts 93 → 97 after 48
  cycles → 99 after 96 — sub-linear, matched by controls under the same driver (`clip` 93 → 95, base 178 → 181), and a rest re-read in the same batch
  reads 93. Contrast the disqualified hover arm at +6.3 MB *per event*, to 394 MB.
- **Wrap fingerprint.** Prose blocks (`h1`, `p0`…`p10`) are **byte-identical to base** on both engines, and the cross-arm diff at every zoom step
  names `pre` as the only differing block. The `<pre>` signature goes `"0"` → `"0,38,76,113,150,181,216,254"` — **1 line → 8** — `offsetHeight`
  143 → 643 K-px (**+167 visual px per code block**), `scrollWidth` 6257 → 1044. It is **zoom-stable**: identical at 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 3 / 1 on both corpora.
  One reflow on upgrade, then no drift.
- **No code change is implied.** `relayout()`'s `ceil(natH·z/K)` held in 30/30 rows across every arm and zoom; `scrollHeight == sizerHeight` exactly;
  the scroll fraction round-trips, its worst drift being integer-`scrollTop` quantisation at z = 0.5 — *smaller* for the wrapping arms than for
  `clip`, a taller document having more scroll range. `proseNatH` is refreshed by the existing ResizeObserver, `savedScrollFrac` is a ratio, and the
  `scrollHeight > clientHeight` guard is satisfied more often.
- **`word-break: break-word` is required, not optional.** `pre-wrap` alone hides overflow rather than preventing it: with a 200-char unbreakable token
  — a path, a URL, base64, a minified line, all common in agent-authored markdown — `wrapx` gives scrollWidth **4693** against clientWidth 1044, still
  4.5× overflow, clipped at the card edge and unreachable: *the same UX as `clip` for that line.* `wrap` reaches `sw == cw == 1044`, free in memory
  and four more lines of layout. **The premise "long code lines wrap instead of being cut off" is FALSE without it.**
- **Nothing escapes the card.** A fixed 34×310 board-background column right of card 0 is byte-identical (sha `7a03c366…`) across all eight arms,
  including `wrapx` with a 200-char token, while a sensitivity control strip inside the card in the same band shows six distinct shas — so the null
  result is not a dead instrument.
- **Correction: the shipped build's "ratchet" is not one.** The 157 → 217 MB growth on scroll reported earlier in this work is **position-dependent,
  not monotonic** — it returns to 157.41 at scroll fraction 0. It is a scroll-position-dependent working set, not a leak, and every earlier
  characterisation of it as a ratchet or a leak is withdrawn. That weakens rather than voids the "bonus bug fixed" framing: the chosen arm's working
  set is lower and flatter, but it repairs no leak, because there was none.

### Rejected and disqualified alternatives — MEASURED

Each was measured, and each fails for a structural reason rather than for want of tuning.

| option | verdict | why, mechanically | the deciding number |
|---|---|---|---|
| `overflow: clip` | runner-up | de-promotes identically, but truncates a long code line at the prose edge where nobody can reach it | 76 / 93 MB — 6 MB cheaper than the chosen arm on SHORT, equal on LONG |
| `overflow: hidden` + a JS wheel→`scrollLeft` handler | rejected | `hidden` keeps a scrollable overflow area `clip` does not, so JS can drive it — but a native wheel does *not* scroll a `hidden` box, making the handler load-bearing, and it reaches wheel/trackpad users only, never keyboard-only ones | 77 / 95 MB; the only spelling that adds JS, and it left per-tick repaint cost and trackpad-vs-board-pan latching UNKNOWN |
| `pre:hover { overflow: auto }` | **disqualified** | each promote↔de-promote transition of a *tiled* scaler leaks; [below](#the-cost-has-one-obvious-repair-and-it-is-disqualified--measured) | 93 MB at rest → **394 MB** after 48 hovers |
| Arm A — prose `zoom` = board zoom (#76 as issued) | **falsified** | a changing root zoom re-runs layout at every zoom value, and WebKit does not scale glyph advances linearly across them | seven distinct layouts over nine zoom steps; `savedScrollFrac` spread ≈ 13 lines |
| Arm B — frozen `zoom: K` + outer `scale(z/K)` | rejected | a CSS tidy-up, not a simplification: `relayout()` survives with only the divisor changed, and `.doc-prose-sizer` is still required because clipping a transformed element happens in its pre-scale space | no measured memory benefit, against a silent 3×-short hazard (below) and a one-time wrap shift (`li2` breaks at char 51 under `zoom: 3` vs 46 under manual ×3) |
| `content-visibility: auto` | not viable | skips layout and paint for off-screen content but leaves the *box* intact, and the box is what sizes the layer | `Layer.memory` byte-identical, 18.38 MB per card either way |
| windowing with spacers | not viable | preserves the box **by construction** — preserving scroll geometry is exactly what the spacers are for | compositedBounds 1176 × 1910 with windowing vs 1176 × 1813 at base |
| lower K (2 or 2.5) | still open — [Decisions](#decisions) §2 | attacks the multiplier, not the promotion; costs `MAX_ZOOM` 3 → 2 and a one-time reflow of all prose | 148 / 198 MB SHORT, but against the *promoted* base, so it does not stack on de-promotion |

Neither `content-visibility` nor windowing attacks K² on *visible* content, where the bytes are in the worst case; only de-promotion does.
**Arm B's hazard is kept because it is silent:** on a zoomed element `offsetHeight` returns pre-zoom own-space units (1099) while
`getBoundingClientRect().height` returns screen space (natH × z), so keeping the shipped `natH * zoom / K` alongside `zoom: K` computes exactly 3×
short at every zoom — the scrollbar lies, two thirds of the document is unreachable, and there is **no error anywhere**.

### The cost has one obvious repair, and it is disqualified — MEASURED

Scoping `overflow: auto` to `:hover` is the first repair a reader will reach for. The scoping itself works — exactly one `.doc-prose-scaler` is
promoted at a time, and the resting cost is `clip`'s cost to the megabyte. What it also does is ratchet.

> Hover-scoped `overflow: auto` holds the cheap state at rest, but each
> promote↔de-promote transition of a tiled `.doc-prose-scaler` grows the WebContent
> footprint by ~6.3 MB that is not returned within 30 s of settle — linear to 394 MB
> after 48 hovers on a 6-card long-doc board whose resting cost is 93 MB — so it
> trades a bounded cost for an unbounded one.

The slope holds within 4% across every dose (6 / 12 / 24 / 48 events) with no plateau, and three controls isolate the cost to the *transition*:
`clip` + 48 no-op class toggles → 94 MB; always-on `auto` + 48 no-op toggles → 177 MB; `clip` + 48 **real** promotions → 394 MB. SHORT is immune (+3 MB over
48 events) — the defect is specific to *tiled* layers, the long-document case this change exists for. `:focus-within` is no escape (a `<pre>` is not
focusable; `tabindex="0"` makes every code block a tab stop and turns the promotion sticky), and neither is "just don't hover much": panning the board
with the cursor parked over prose fires one promote and one de-promote per card crossed.

**Detecting it required a process-level instrument.** Engine A's `LayerTree` reported layer memory returning *exactly* — 14 → 16 → 14 layers across
cycles, zero accumulation; only `footprint -p` on the real WKWebView saw the growth. The arms differ in both engine build and instrument, so which
explains the disagreement is unresolved — REASONED: the warning survives either answer, and [Verification](#verification) §2 is written for the
instrument that would have missed the defect. (Hover on real WKWebView is a JS-class proxy, validated byte-identical to `:hover` on Engine A first.)

## Costs

### R1 — the new invariant is load-bearing and silently reversible

"No composited descendant inside `.doc-prose-scaler`" becomes a real invariant of the doc card, and **markdown is user content**. Raw HTML containing
`will-change` or a 3D transform re-promotes the subtree: MEASURED **59.69 MB → 238.55 MB** on Engine A (read the 4× ratio, not the levels — the card
count and corpus behind that pair were not recorded). REASONED: the failure mode degrades to *today's* cost and never worse, and it is silent — no
error, no visual difference, only memory. Promotion is also an engine heuristic observed on these builds, not a specced contract, so a WebKit update
is a second way it can flip with no code change here. It is not Vitest-testable — `docZoom.test.ts` S12 reads `card.css` from disk and asserts on rule
text, which could pin the declarations but never observe promotion — so the honest instrument is a QA probe page alongside the existing ones in
`desktop/qa/`.

### R2 — crispness on a 1× display is unverified — UNKNOWN

A real compositor capture of the `clip` arm reads 1.2% mean luminance difference and **−1.6% sharpness** against base — pixel-aligned, so real rather
than a registration artifact, and visually indistinguishable at 2× (REASONED to carry to the chosen arm, whose layers are byte-identical outside
`.doc-scroll`). But that delta has no calibration: a valid composited K=1 capture could not be obtained, so there is no reference for how much of it
is inherent to compositing versus caused by de-promotion. The check that settles it is `make run` on a true 1× panel, the same instrument
[`../../desktop/qa/one-x-display-crispness.md`](../../desktop/qa/one-x-display-crispness.md) used. Until then "indistinguishable at 2×" is the
strongest claim available, and it is a 2× claim only.

### R3 — gesture smoothness is unverified in the real app — UNKNOWN

Frame timing was measured headless under a 30 fps cap — p99 38 ms for `clip` against base's 49 ms, which is "no regression detected", not a win.
Whether a pinch-zoom over a board of de-promoted doc cards *feels* the same needs confirmation in the running app, not the probe.

### What is explicitly NOT bought

**Issue #76's second goal — unlocking `MAX_ZOOM` > 3 — is not delivered.** The crispness ceiling remains tied to K by `docZoom.test.ts` S13
(`DOC_OVERSAMPLE_K >= MAX_ZOOM`, importing the live `MAX_ZOOM` from `BoardEngine`, and `MAX_ZOOM / DOC_OVERSAMPLE_K <= 1`), untouched by this design.
Whether de-promotion *changes* the z > K upscale behaviour is **UNKNOWN and unmeasured**: when the prose paints into the scroller's layer instead of a
cached K× raster, the thing being upscaled above z = K may not be the same thing — it might be better, it might be worse. Nothing here licenses a
claim either way, and nobody should raise `MAX_ZOOM` on the strength of this doc.

## Decisions

1. **Which spelling — DECIDED (2026-08-28): `overflow: visible; white-space: pre-wrap; word-break: break-word`.** Closed by a human; recorded here, no
   longer open.

   Three spellings de-promote identically and the spread between them is small and now measured: `clip` 76 / 93 MB, the chosen arm 81 / 93 MB,
   `hidden`+JS 77 / 95 MB. **Memory was therefore never the separating axis. Reachability was.** Under the chosen arm every reader sees every
   character; under `hidden`+JS a wheel or trackpad user reaches the overflow and a keyboard-only user does not, because the box is not focusable and,
   being `overflow: hidden`, never renders a scrollbar to say it scrolls at all; under `clip` nobody reaches it. A clipped code line in an agent's
   report is content the reader cannot recover, and buying that back is what the +6 MB on short docs and the one-time re-wrap of code blocks are spent
   on. `word-break: break-word` is part of the decision, not a garnish: without it the chosen arm collapses back to `clip`'s behaviour on exactly the
   lines that matter ([Finding 8](#finding-8--the-chosen-arm-end-to-end--measured)).

   **What a spec must now assert**, and it differs from what `clip` would have asked for: prose blocks byte-identical to base, `<pre>`
   **deliberately** re-wrapped once (1 → 8 lines on the probe corpus, +167 visual px per block), the whole fingerprint zoom-stable across 0.5 / 1 / 2
   / 3 / 1, and the scroll fraction round-tripping — and **not** a constant `scrollHeight` ([above](#a-premise-correction-a-spec-must-not-inherit)).

2. **Whether to also lower K — STILL OPEN.** Independent of the de-promotion, but **it does not simply stack**: the −112 MB measured for K=2 is
   against the *promoted* shipped base, and this design's own root cause says the K× backing store exists only because the scaler is promoted, so once
   de-promoted the marginal saving from a smaller K should be far smaller. **How much smaller is unmeasured, and is not estimated here.** Lowering K
   still costs `MAX_ZOOM` 3 → 2 and a one-time reflow of all prose. *Leaning no for now* — de-promotion alone takes short docs from 261 MB to 81 MB,
   and lowering K spends board range users already have to buy a saving nobody has measured.

3. **Whether Arm B's CSS tidy-up is worth its hazard — decided: no.** No measured benefit, against a silent 3×-short failure and a one-time wrap
   shift; see the [alternatives table](#rejected-and-disqualified-alternatives--measured).

## Verification

Not "memory looks lower". What makes the result non-vacuous, in the spirit of 2608.0001's control arm:

1. **Wrap invariance, positively and negatively**, with [`../../desktop/qa/doc-prose-zoom-probe.html`](../../desktop/qa/doc-prose-zoom-probe.html) —
   character offsets per block, not line counts, over a 0.5 → 3 → 0.5 sweep. The assertion is *prose byte-identical, `<pre>` re-wrapped once and then
   stable*, not "nothing changed"; Arm A stays in as the negative control.
2. **Promotion, observed** — a QA page rendering a doc card's real DOM, reporting whether `.doc-prose-scaler` has its own composited layer. Not `ps -o
   rss=`, blind here (Problem §1); and **not `LayerTree` alone**, which reported zero accumulation for the hover ratchet `footprint -p` measured at
   +301 MB. The standing detector must read process footprint beside layer state, on real WKWebView.
3. **Process memory** — `footprint -p`, six cards, both corpora, against the canonical table. Finding 8 covers z = 1 including the scroll pass but
   **not z = 0.5**, so the 260-vs-246 base gap between earlier sessions is still open. **Pin the window to a screen with `backingScaleFactor ≥ 2`
   first**, and record the configuration beside every figure.
4. **Crispness on a true 1× panel** (R2) by eye under `make run`, and **gesture feel** (R3) in the real app. Nothing in `make test` will catch a
   regression of the promotion invariant — a fact about the instrument, not a reason to skip it.

## What this supersedes

**Issue #76's mechanism, not its problem.** The memory case stands and is understated; the implementation does not survive measurement, and its
acceptance criteria need rewriting. "Wrap points unchanged across zoom" *dies as written* (seven layouts, nine steps) and must be restated, not
deleted: prose unchanged, code blocks re-wrapped **once** by design, fingerprint then zoom-stable. "`savedScrollFrac` survives the migration" *dies as
written* — there is no migration left to survive. "On zoom-incapable WebKit: K× path untouched" and "S13 applies only to the fallback path" are
*void*: the macOS 26 floor leaves no such target, [#94](https://github.com/pingeplin/tarmac/issues/94) removes the probe they assume, and S13 applies
unchanged to the one and only path. "Zooming past K stays crisp" is *not delivered and not falsified* ([above](#what-is-explicitly-not-bought)) — the
one #76 goal still open. "Doc-card raster memory at zoom ≤ 1 drops to ~1× area (measured)" *survives, and is the criterion worth keeping*.

**2608.0001 is corrected on one point:** it says #76 "remains deferred pending its own triggers", but trigger 1 ("memory profiling shows doc-card
raster cost hurts on real boards") has now fired, and the fix it triggers is not the one #76 names. **The K× model itself is untouched** —
`DOC_OVERSAMPLE_K`, `MAGNIFY_K`, `scale(zoom/K)` and 2608.0001's reasoning all stand: a K× *layout* need not imply a K× *backing store*.
