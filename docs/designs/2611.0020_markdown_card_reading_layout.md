## Owner decisions (2026-11-21)

These bind the design. The owner approved each one.

- **O1. Text column.** Body text has a maximum width of 46em (644px at the standard 14px). It is centered in the space beside the sidebar.
- **O2. Wide blocks.** Tables and code blocks can be wider than the text, up to 1040px, centered. When they are still too wide, they scroll sideways in their own box. A text column of a table keeps a minimum width of about 220px.
- **O3. Table style.** A rounded 1px outer border, row lines only, a header row of 11.5–12px muted text on a light background (theme tokens `bg2`, `line`), cell padding about 8px 12px. Inline code in a cell does not wrap.
- **O4. Front matter.** It is parsed out and is not shown in the body. The sidebar shows it as key/value rows; a list (for example `tags`) shows as chips.
- **O5. Sidebar (option A).** Front matter on top, the outline (the document's headings) below. The current section is marked with the agent accent. A click on an entry scrolls to that heading. Drag the inner edge to resize (160–420, standard 240). A drag below 160 collapses it to a 30px strip with two icons (`{}` front matter, `≡` outline) and heading ticks. A double-click on the edge sets 240. On a wide card it is open by default and pushes the text, which re-wraps. On a card of the standard width (392) it is closed by default, and a strip icon slides the panel over the text without a re-wrap. Each card keeps its own width and open state.
- **O6. Setting.** A new Settings pane "Documents" after Fonts and Theme; group "Markdown cards"; row "Sidebar side" with two picture tiles "Left" and "Right" (the selected tile has a blue border); help text "The sidebar holds the front matter and the outline." Standard: Left. Stored in `app-prefs.json`. A change moves the sidebar of every open markdown card at once and keeps each card's width and open state. The pane icon is an SF Symbol, as on the other panes.

## Review decisions (advisor, 2026-11-21)

- **A1. Markup from marked's tokens, not the DOM.** `doc-layout.js` renders through its own `marked.Marked` instance: a `table` renderer override writes the wrapper, and a `walkTokens` hook marks the text-column cells that the cell renderer gives the class (D3, D5), and the outline comes from `heading` tokens of the same parse (D9). So every rule is tested markdown-in, HTML-out in a bare `JSContext`; no TDD exception is taken. A raw HTML table or heading is not a token of that kind: it gets no wrapper and is not in the outline.
- **A2.** Nested wide blocks stay in their container; only a direct child of `.doc-prose` breaks out (D2). Lines are `1px·K` (D4). An East Asian wide character counts 2 (D5).
- **A3.** Overlay width `[min(160, cardWidth − 30), min(420, cardWidth − 30)]` (D10). On the Right, sidebar content keeps 12px clear of the outer edge, for the card's scroll thumb (D14). The sidebar is outside `.doc-scroll` and scrolls on its own with no bar (D13). The outline keeps element references, not ids (D9). Field values go in as `textContent` (D8). A drag re-wraps live; QA measures a long doc; re-wrap on release is the fallback (D11). A collapse keeps the width from before the drag and stores open = false (D11, D16). The zoom range is 0.1–3.

## Problem

A markdown doc card is hard to read on a wide card, and the front matter is unreadable.

- **Width.** `.doc-prose` has `max-width: 720px` and is left-aligned (`DocTemplate.html`). On a 1280-wide card, about 560px on the right is empty. The research (board `Ref`) shows that every reader limits the measure and centers it (Pandoc 36em, GitHub 980px, Claude Docs about 720px). Claude Artifacts pages use a narrow text column and wider tables.
- **Tables.** The page has no table CSS. A wide table either squeezes its text columns to two or three words per line or runs out of the card.
- **Front matter.** marked renders a YAML front matter block as an `<hr>` and then one setext `<h2>` with all the keys run together. No reader in the research shows it like that.

## Constraints from the code

- **Zoom is a pure view transform.** The prose is laid out once at K times its size (`--oversample-k: 3`) and scaled down by `.doc-prose-scaler`. Only a card resize or a font change re-wraps it. Every new box (the sidebar too) follows this rule: its layout is a function of the card width, the sidebar width and the fonts, never of the zoom. Every new px length in the page is written times K (the link underline's bare `1px` predates this).
- **The page runs no script from the doc** (`script-src 'none'`). `doc-render.js` is the app's own script and runs in the app's content world. New interaction (drag, click) lives there.
- **Press routing.** The app judges a press before the page sees it (`AppController.handlePress`, `CardPress`). A press on a doc body selects the card and then reaches the page, as text selection does today. A press on a link is the only exception (`pointerIsOverLink`).
- **No colour literals.** `DocTemplateTests` S17 forbids `#hex`, `rgb(` and `rgba(` in the template; every colour is a theme token. Today `ThemeCSS.properties` gives the page only `--bg1`, `--bg2`, `--term-bg`, `--text`, `--prose-text`, `--agent`, `--agent-dim` and `color-scheme`. `Palette` has `line`, `muted` and `scrollThumb`, but the page does not get them. So slice 1 adds the tokens that D4 and D7 use to `ThemeCSS.properties`, test-first in `ThemeCSSTests` (spec 2611.0021 D8).
- **Kit-first.** The `TarmacApp` shell has no unit tests. Pure decisions go to `TarmacKit` with XCTest. `doc-render.js` has no test suite today; its new rules go in a DOM-free script that JavaScriptCore runs under XCTest, as `CardShimTests` does for `card_shim.js` and `CardHostScriptTests` for `card-host.js`. That suite tests the pure rules only. The drag, click and press wiring in the page has no automated test; QA through `tarmac dev` checks it.
- **Out of scope.** `card_shim.js`, `card-host.js` and the HTML card (issue #441 works there). A front matter `title` shown as a heading (see Alternatives). A full YAML parser.

## Decision

Four slices, one issue, branch and PR each, in this order. Each slice ships alone.

1. **Slice 1 (#443) — column and wide blocks.** CSS in `DocTemplate.html`, plus a new DOM-free `doc-layout.js` that renders each table in a scroll box and marks its text columns. Spec: `.blueprint/specs/2611.0021_doc_reading_column.md`, which is normative for slice 1 where it is more exact than this page.
2. **Slice 2 (#444) — front matter and the sidebar.** `TarmacKit` splits the front matter off the markdown and owns the sidebar's mode and size rules. The sidebar is part of the doc page: its own K-times layout, built and driven by `doc-render.js`. State is in memory only.
3. **Slice 3 (#445) — per-card state.** Two optional keys on the layout `Tile`, so a card's sidebar width and open state survive a restart.
4. **Slice 4 (#446) — the Documents pane.** A `SettingsPane` case, an `app-prefs.json` key, a pane with two picture tiles, and a broadcast to every open markdown card.

**The trade-off in one sentence:** the sidebar is HTML inside the doc page, not an AppKit view, so the outline always matches the headings that marked rendered and the sidebar scales with the card for free; the cost is a page script that now handles a drag.

## Key assumptions

- **A press on the page can start a drag on the same press that selects the card.** The app's local mouse-down monitor (`AppController+Keys.swift`) calls `handlePress` and returns the event, so the page gets the press after the select. Text selection on an unselected card works this way today. D11 depends on this. If it breaks, see the fallback in *Risks*.
- **marked's `heading` tokens are the outline.** D9 takes the outline from the same parse that renders the page, so they cannot disagree.
- **A sideways scroll box works inside the scaled K-times layout.** D2, D6 and D7 depend on it. The slice 1 spike checks it (D7).

## Approach

### Slice 1 — the column and wide blocks

- **D1. The column.** The column width is `min(46em, available)`, with `em` the prose's own font size, so the measure follows the Document font size. It is defined once, on `.doc-prose`, so the font size of a child (a heading, code) cannot change it. Preferred form: `.doc-prose` keeps normal block flow and gets an inline padding that centers a 46em column (`max(22px·K, (width − 46em) / 2)`), so the vertical margins of blocks still collapse as today. A CSS grid (`1fr min(46em, 100%) 1fr`) is the fallback; it stops margin collapse and needs explicit block spacing. The implementation picks one after a spike and records why. The spike also checks that a D2 block breaks out of the column and centers in the chosen form.
- **D2. Wide blocks.** A table (in its wrapper, D3) and a `pre` break out of the column to `min(1040px·K, the prose width less its minimum padding)`, centered. A block whose content fits the column stays at the column width: a wide block is never narrower than the column and grows only when its content needs the room. Content wider than the cap scrolls sideways inside the block. When the column is wider than the cap (Document size 23px and up), the column wins. Only a direct child of `.doc-prose` breaks out; a table or code block in a list item or blockquote stays in its container and scrolls in its own box (A2).
- **D3. Table wrapper.** marked emits a bare `<table>`. `doc-layout.js` renders each `table` token as `<div class="doc-table">` around marked's default markup (A1). A raw HTML table is untouched. The wrapper is the scroll box and draws the rounded border, so the border does not scroll away.
- **D4. Table style.** Border-collapse; every line `1px·K`; a rounded outer border in `line`; a row line between body rows; no vertical lines. Header cells: font size 0.82em of the prose (11.5px at 14px), weight 500, `muted`, background `bg2`, `nowrap`. Cell padding 8px·K by 12px·K, left-aligned unless marked wrote an `align`, top-aligned. Every cell rule selects inside `.doc-table`. `code` in a cell: `white-space: nowrap`.
- **D5. Text columns.** A rule in the DOM-free script (`doc-layout.js`, shared with slice 2) marks a column as a text column when some body cell has a text width of 40 or more outside inline code (an East Asian wide character counts 2), read from the cell's inline tokens. The table renderer gives every cell of such a column the class `doc-text-col`, which has `min-width: 220px·K`. Other columns get no minimum.
- **D6. Code blocks.** `pre` changes from `pre-wrap` (wraps) to `pre` with sideways scroll inside the block. This is a behaviour change: a long code line no longer wraps.
- **D7. The scroll affordance.** The doc's own scroll bar is hidden (the card draws its thumb). A sideways scroll box shows a thin, always-visible scroll bar styled with `::-webkit-scrollbar` in theme tokens, so the reader sees that there is more. The spike checks that WKWebView draws it inside the scaled layout and that a sideways wheel over a focused card scrolls it.

### Slice 2 — front matter and the sidebar

- **D8. Front matter split (`TarmacKit`, `DocFrontMatter`).** A block is front matter only when line 1 is exactly `---` (trailing spaces and a CR allowed), a later line is `---` or `...`, and at least one line between them is `key: …` at column 0 (key: letters, digits, `_`, `-`). Otherwise the markdown is unchanged, so a doc that opens with a thematic break and no `key:` line renders as today. Cost: a doc that opens with `---`, a line such as `Note: text`, and `---` is a thematic break and a setext heading today; with this rule that heading moves to the sidebar as the field `Note`. Parsed fields, in order: `key: value` (one pair of surrounding quotes removed); `key: [a, b]` (a list); `key:` followed by `- item` lines (a list); any other line is kept as raw text under the key above it. No YAML library. The body handed to marked is the text after the closing line. `DocWebView` passes the body and the fields to `tarmacDoc.render`. The page writes field keys and values with `textContent`, never as HTML (A3).
- **D9. The outline comes from marked's `heading` tokens.** `doc-layout.js` returns the `h1`–`h3` tokens of the same parse with the HTML, and the page pairs each with its rendered element in order (it keeps element references, not ids). Owner's list said "outline extraction in TarmacKit"; this design deviates on purpose: a second markdown parser in Swift can disagree with marked (setext headings, headings in lists), and then a click scrolls to the wrong heading. A raw HTML heading is not in the outline. The rule for the current section (the last heading whose top is at or above the reading line) is a pure function in `doc-layout.js`, tested under JavaScriptCore.
- **D10. Sidebar mode (`TarmacKit`, `DocSidebar`).** Inputs: the card width (world px), whether the doc has front matter or headings, the stored width and the stored open state (nil = no choice yet). Output: `none`, `strip`, `docked(width)` or `overlay(width)`.
  - No front matter and no headings → `none`: no sidebar, no strip; the text has the whole card.
  - A card is wide when `cardWidth − 240 ≥ 392` (632 or more): the standard sidebar leaves at least a standard card's width for the text. Both numbers already exist (`Placement.docSize.width`, the sidebar standard).
  - Wide and open (the default when no choice is stored) → `docked`, width clamped to `[160, min(420, cardWidth − 392)]`. Wide and closed → `strip`, and a strip icon opens `docked`. Not wide → `strip`, and a strip icon opens `overlay`, width clamped to `[min(160, cardWidth − 30), min(420, cardWidth − 30)]` (a card can be 160 wide, `CardResize.minWidth`).
  - A card resized across 632 changes the mode, never the stored state. A clamp changes the width in use, never the stored width.
  - All the numbers (160, 240, 420, 30, 392) are in `DocSidebar` and are passed to the page with each layout, so they are written once.
- **D11. Drag and reset.** The grip is the sidebar's inner edge, inside the page. A press on it selects the card (as any body press does) and starts the drag; the page stops the press from starting a text selection. The page divides the pointer's screen delta by the zoom (widths are world px), clamps live with the limits it was given, and below 160 shows the strip. On release it posts the result (`docSidebar {width, open}`); `DocSidebar` validates it as `ScrollMetrics(report:)` does, and a report it refuses changes nothing. A double-click on the grip sets 240 and open. A drag that collapses keeps the width from before the drag and sets open = false. The prose re-wraps live during a docked drag; slice 2 QA measures a long doc, and re-wrap on release (the sidebar over the text while dragging) is the fallback.
- **D12. Overlay.** In the overlay the text does not re-wrap. A click on the active strip icon or in the text closes it. An overlay that is open is never stored (slice 3): a narrow card always comes back with the strip.
- **D13. K-times layout.** The sidebar has its own sizer and scaler, as the prose has, so a zoom does not re-wrap it. The prose's laid-out width becomes the card width less the space the mode takes (`docked`: the sidebar and the grip; `strip` and `overlay`: 30). The sidebar is outside `.doc-scroll` and scrolls on its own with the wheel, with no visible bar.
- **D14. Strip.** Two icons, `{}` (opens the panel at the front matter, only when there is front matter) and `≡` (at the outline). Below them, one tick per `h1`/`h2`, the current one in `agent`. The icons sit at least 6px from the card's outer edge, which is the resize handle zone (`CardHandles.edgeThickness`). On the Right, sidebar content keeps 12px clear of the outer edge, where the card's scroll thumb is (`ScrollIndicator`: 10px wide, 2px inset).

### Slice 3 — per-card state on the tile

- **D15. Tile keys.** `Tile` gets `sidebar_w: float | nil` and `sidebar_open: bool | nil`, both optional and written only on doc tiles that have a choice. Additive: both codecs, the conformance vectors, `protocol.md` and `architecture.md`. `Registry::set_tiles` keeps a tile whole and `persist.rs` writes `registry.tiles` with serde, so the daemon stores the keys with no logic change (checked: `core/crates/tarmacd/src/boards.rs:338`, `persist.rs:162`). The app side does change: `LayoutTiles.build` (`TarmacKit`) builds each doc tile from a `DocInput`, which must carry the two values, and the restore path reads them back into the card.
- **D16. What is stored.** The width after a drag or a reset (a collapse keeps the width from before the drag). The open state only after the user opened or closed the docked sidebar; an overlay never writes it.

### Slice 4 — the Documents pane

- **D17. Pref.** `AppPrefs.Values.docSidebarSide` (`left` standard, `right`), key `doc_sidebar_side`, written only when `right`, so a file with no choice keeps its bytes (`AppPrefsTests`).
- **D18. Pane.** `SettingsPane.documents` after `theme` (title "Documents", SF Symbol `doc.text`). Group "Markdown cards", row "Sidebar side", two picture tiles in the look of `ThemeTile`, the help text of O6.
- **D19. Live change.** A `DocSidebarSideFollowing` protocol, in the shape of `ThemeFollowing`: every open markdown card is told the new side through `AppController.tellEveryView`, as a theme change is, so the cards on boards that are not mounted are told too. Each card passes it to its page, which moves the sidebar and keeps its width and open state.

## Alternatives

- **The sidebar as an AppKit view beside the web view.** Native drag and cursor, and the rules stay in Swift. Rejected: it needs its own text drawing for the front matter and outline, its own zoom handling, and a round trip to the page for every outline click and every scroll (the current section).
- **The outline parsed in Swift from the source.** Testable with XCTest. Rejected (D9): it can disagree with marked.
- **Per-card state in an app-side file keyed by path.** No wire change. Rejected: it needs its own keys and cleanup when a card closes, and path keys are already fragile (#314). The tile has the card's lifecycle.
- **Front matter as chips under the title in the body** (board `RecMd`). Replaced by the sidebar by the owner.
- **Front matter `title` as a heading when the doc has no `h1`.** Not now: the body stays what marked renders from the file. It can come later.

## Success criteria

- On a 1280-wide card with no docked sidebar, at zoom 1, the text column is 644px wide (at 14px) and centered. A table whose content is wider than 1040px is 1040px wide, centered, and scrolls sideways in its box; a table that fits the column is the column's width (slice 1). With a docked sidebar, the cap is the D2 formula on the prose width of D13.
- A table column with a body cell of 40 or more characters outside inline code is at least 220px wide (D5). A long code line does not wrap and scrolls sideways (D6) (slice 1).
- A doc that opens with YAML front matter shows no `<hr>` and no run-together heading; its keys are in the sidebar. A doc that opens with `---` and has no `key:` line before the next `---` renders as today (slice 2).
- Rules, by XCTest and JavaScriptCore tests: the `DocSidebar` modes of D10 (wide/narrow at 631 and 632, the clamps, a width below 160 gives `strip`), the validation of a `docSidebar` report (D11), the outline from heading tokens and the current-section rule of D9 (slice 2).
- Wiring, by QA through `tarmac dev`: a drag of the grip resizes the sidebar and below 160 shows the strip; a double-click sets 240 and open; an overlay opens and closes and does not re-wrap the text; a click on an outline entry scrolls to its heading (slice 2).
- A zoom from 0.1 to 3 moves no line break in the body or the sidebar (all slices; QA through `tarmac dev`).
- A card's sidebar width and open state survive an app restart and a daemon restart (slice 3).
- Choosing Right in Settings moves the sidebar of every open markdown card at once (slice 4).

## Risks and open items

- **Press routing on an unselected card** (D11): the press selects the card and then reaches the page. A live check in slice 2 confirms the drag starts on that same press. If it does not, `CardPress` gets a `.docControl` place reported by the page, as `.link` is. `CardPress.selects` is false for such a place, so in that fallback a press on the grip does not select the card, and D11 changes to match.
- **Sideways scroll in the scaled layout** (D7), **margin collapse and breakout** (D1) and **`min-width` on a table cell** (D5): spikes at the start of slice 1, recorded in its PR.
