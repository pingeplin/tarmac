# 2609.0013 Whole-pixel relayed scroll deltas for HTML cards

**Date:** 2026-09-09
**Status:** accepted
**Author:** EP Lin

## Context

Scrolling an HTML doc card leaves an intermittent **pure-white band** across the
card's full width, at the top or bottom edge of the card body ([#142]). The band
covers content that exists and clears on its own after a few seconds.

The white is `.html-frame`'s own base (`desktop/src/theme/card.css:339-343`,
`background: #fff`) showing through iframe tiles WebKit never painted — confirmed
by swapping that backdrop to magenta and watching the band turn magenta.

The trigger is **fractional scroll deltas reaching the card document**. The wheel
relay converts a screen-px delta into the document's own units with
`cardScrollDelta` (`desktop/src/kit/cardZoom.ts:30-32`), which divides by board
zoom in magnify mode, and the shim executes the result verbatim as
`window.scrollBy` (`desktop/src-tauri/src/card_shim.js:284-286`). At any
non-integer board zoom every relayed delta is therefore fractional — at zoom
1.728 a 60 px wheel notch becomes 34.72.

Measured, same geometry and cadence, varying only the step:

| arm | frames | banded |
|---|---|---|
| `scrollBy(0, 34.72)` | 200 | 126 |
| `scrollBy(0, 35)` | 200 | **0** |
| real wheel through the relay, before | 260 | 204 |
| real wheel through the relay, after | 260 | **0** |

The mechanism beneath that is **inferred, not measured**: WebKit floors the
document's scroll position to an `IntPoint` in the web process while forwarding
the fractional delta to the UI-process scrolling tree, so the two drift apart by
the residue on each event. Why the drift is monotonic under direction reversal,
and why the exposed strip is never repainted, is unexplained. The spec rests on
the measured trigger, not on the mechanism.

## Motivation

Every HTML card at a non-integer board zoom is affected, which is most of them —
the board zooms continuously. The card is Tarmac's primary way of showing an
agent's written output, and a white band over that output reads as data loss.

Doing nothing means the flagship surface visibly corrupts itself during ordinary
reading.

## Proposed Solution

### Overview

Quantize the relayed delta to whole layout pixels before it reaches the document,
carrying the sub-pixel residue into the next event so total travel is conserved
and slow trackpad scrolls do not stall.

This is deliberately **orthogonal to the zoom path**. `MAGNIFY_K`, the K× iframe
box (`cardIframePx`), the `scale(zoom/K)` transform, and the shim's
`documentElement.style.zoom = K` are all untouched. Crispness (the host transform
stays a down-scale) and the no-reflow guarantee (layout still runs once, at the
frozen K) are therefore preserved **by construction**, not by measurement.

### Key Components

- **`quantizeScrollDelta(delta, carry)`** — new pure function in
  `desktop/src/kit/cardZoom.ts`. Returns the whole-px `step` to send and the
  `carry` to feed the next call. Unit-tested in `cardZoom.test.ts`.
- **`HtmlCard` wheel relay** — the wheel `useEffect` in
  `desktop/src/cards/HtmlCard.tsx` (the one keyed on
  `[props.selected, props.borrowed, magnify]`; cited by symbol, not by line, since
  this change moves those lines) — thin wiring: hold the carry per axis across
  events in the effect closure, quantize both axes, post integers.

### Data Flow

1. Wheel event reaches the shield on a selected card.
2. `cardScrollDelta` converts screen px to the document's units (unchanged).
3. `quantizeScrollDelta` splits that into a whole-px step plus a residue.
4. The residue is retained for the next event; the whole-px step is posted. A
   step of 0 on *both* axes is not posted at all — `scrollBy(0, 0)` is a no-op,
   so suppressing it changes nothing observable and spares the message.
5. The shim calls `window.scrollBy` with an integer.

Step 3 sits *after* the mode conversion, so it runs on both zoom modes. In reveal
`cardScrollDelta` is the identity, so the step differs from the delta only when
the incoming wheel delta is itself fractional; the banding case this spec measures
is the magnify one, where the divide-by-zoom makes it fractional every time.

### Interface Contract

```ts
// desktop/src/kit/cardZoom.ts
export function quantizeScrollDelta(
  delta: number,
  carry: number,
): { step: number; carry: number };
```

- **Domain:** `delta` and `carry` are finite numbers — a `WheelEvent` delta always
  is. Behaviour on `NaN`/`Infinity` is unspecified and is not tested.
- `step` is always an integer (`Number.isInteger(step)`), including `-0` for a
  residue-absorbed negative delta.
- **Conservation:** `step + returned carry` equals `delta + incoming carry` to
  within `1e-9`. Exact `===` is *not* required — the sum is floating point.
- **Bounded residue:** `|returned carry| < 1`. The carry is a sub-pixel remainder,
  never a parked whole pixel.
- **The rounding policy is round-to-nearest** (`Math.round` semantics, including
  its asymmetry on negative ties: `−2.5 → −2`). Specified, not left open, on
  behavioural grounds: nearest bounds the per-event error at 0.5 px where
  truncation lags by up to a whole pixel, and scroll feel is what this function
  exists to protect. Pinning it also lets the tests assert values rather than
  mere integrality, which is what kills the truncate/floor/ceil and 2-px-grid
  mutants. (An earlier draft justified the pin by claiming the carry would be
  untestable under an open policy. That was **false** and is recorded here so it
  is not repeated: S4 — a repeated 0.4 px delta must reach 1 px — is entirely
  policy-independent and kills a store-but-never-apply implementation under
  round, truncate and floor alike. Only S5's specific construction is
  policy-dependent.)
- The wire payload `{ tarmac: "scroll", dx, dy }` is unchanged in shape; only its
  values become integral. The shim needs no change, and no protocol version does.

## Alternatives Considered

### Round without carrying the residue

Simpler, but a slow trackpad emits deltas whose per-event value rounds to 0, so
the card would refuse to scroll at all below a threshold. Rejected.

### Quantize inside the shim instead of the host

Would also cover a card author's own fractional `scrollBy`. Rejected for this
change: covering the author case means overriding a visible API
(`window.scrollBy`) inside every card document, which is a semantic change to a
document Tarmac does not own — it deserves its own decision and its own
measurement of the author-initiated case. Tracked below as a known gap.

(The shim is *not* untestable: `desktop/src/card-shim.test.ts` executes the
shipped `card_shim.js` bytes in a Node `vm` and already stubs `scrollBy`. Lack of
a harness is not a reason here, and per `docs/coding-style.md` §1.3 would not
have been one.)

**A middle option this draft did not weigh**, recorded so the next scroll-relay
change need not re-derive it: quantize inside the shim's own `tarmac === "scroll"`
handler, before it calls the native `scrollBy`. That overrides nothing — it is
Tarmac's own script finishing the job on Tarmac's own private message — and it
sits at the actual chokepoint, the single place a relayed value reaches
`scrollBy`. Coverage is identical today (one poster, one handler), so it was not
worth reworking the change for; but under the shipped placement a *second* future
poster of `{tarmac:"scroll"}` gets no protection unless it independently uses
`createScrollRelay`. If such a poster appears, move the invariant to the shim
rather than duplicating the relay.

### Remove the in-document magnification (reveal mode, host-side scrolling)

Both eliminate the band, but reveal reflows on zoom — the thing magnify exists to
prevent — and host-side scrolling changes `100vh`/`position: fixed` semantics and
stops scroll-reactive cards working. Rejected as disproportionate.

## Acceptance Scenarios

Legend: **[K]** desktop kit unit test (Vitest, `desktop/src/kit/cardZoom.test.ts`),
**[D]** diff-review criterion — checked by reading the change, not by an assertion,
**[Q]** manual QA checklist item in a new `desktop/qa/scroll-band-qa.md`.

Throughout, `d` is the delta under test and *the S7 tolerance* is `1e-9`.

### Happy Path
- **S1 [K]:** Given a whole-number delta (including `0`) and no carry, when
  quantized, then the step equals the delta exactly and the returned carry is 0.
- **S2 [K]:** Given a fractional total — a fractional delta with no carry, and
  again an integral delta with a fractional carry — when quantized, then the step
  is the nearest integer to that total — assert the value (35 for 34.72), not
  merely `Number.isInteger`; integrality alone leaves truncate, floor, ceil and a
  2-px grid all passing.

### Edge Cases
- **S3 [K]:** Given a run of N identical fractional deltas (e.g. the measured
  34.72), when each is quantized with the carry the previous call returned, then
  every step is an integer, **every prefix sum is within 1 px of that prefix's
  exact travel** — `|Σsteps_n − n·d| ≤ 1` for every n ≤ N, so the error is bounded
  and does not grow with n. The bound is stated as `≤ 1` rather than the
  `≤ 0.5 + ε` that round-to-nearest actually achieves, so the scenario keeps
  asserting "bounded, not growing" rather than re-deriving the policy — and `Σsteps_N + carry_N` equals `N·d` to within the
  S7 tolerance. This is the no-drift assertion: a carry-less implementation is off
  by ~0.28 px per event at this delta and so breaks the prefix bound by the fourth
  event. Compute `n·d`; do not hard-code the expected totals.
- **S4 [K]:** Given a repeated delta smaller than half a pixel (e.g. 0.4 px), when
  quantized in sequence with the carry, then the accumulated steps reach at least
  1 px within `⌈1/d⌉ + 1` events and keep growing thereafter — a slow trackpad
  scroll advances instead of stalling. (The `+ 1` is float slack, not laxity: under
  a truncating policy a run of 0.1 accumulates to `0.9999999999999999` at event 10
  and truncates to 0.)
- **S5 [K]:** Given the carry returned by quantizing a downward delta, when a
  **small** upward delta is quantized with that carry — one that would round to a
  step of 0 on its own, so that only the inherited residue can tip it past a whole
  pixel — then the step is that whole pixel. This is the assertion that proves the
  carry is *consumed* rather than merely stored. (An *equal-and-opposite* reversal
  cannot prove it: for any non-half `d`, `round(−d + (d − round(d)))` is `−round(d)`
  and the residue cancels to 0 whether or not the carry was applied, so an
  implementation that tracks the carry and never uses it passes. Do not "simplify"
  this scenario back to equal magnitudes.) Additionally, an equal-and-opposite
  reversal must net exactly 0 — weaker, but it is the no-compounding claim.
- **S6 [K]:** Given a negative delta of magnitude **at least 1 px** and no carry,
  when quantized, then the step is a negative integer — direction is preserved.
  (The bound is load-bearing: a −0.4 px delta may legitimately quantize to a step
  of 0 with the residue carried, so the sign claim only holds above 1 px.)

### Contract Invariants
- **S7 [K]:** Given any finite delta and carry, when quantized, then
  `step + carry_out` equals `delta + carry_in` to within `1e-9` — no travel is
  created or destroyed. Cover the input *classes*, not one value: zero, integral,
  fractional, negative, and large (≥ 1000 px) deltas, each against a zero, a
  positive-fractional and a negative-fractional incoming carry.
- **S9 [K]:** *(numbered out of sequence — added at the spec-review gate, so
  S1–S8 keep their numbers.)* Given any
  finite delta and carry, when quantized, then
  `|carry_out| < 1` — the residue is always sub-pixel. This is the invariant the
  Trade-offs rely on when the relay's carry resets, and it is what rules out an
  implementation that satisfies S7 by parking whole pixels in the carry and never
  scrolling.

### Relay step (added at the second referee gate)

The wheel handler was found to carry three decisions the kit did not cover — it
holds per-axis state across events, its statement ordering is load-bearing, and it
chooses whether to post at all. Mutating any of them left the whole suite green.
They move into `relayScrollStep` in `desktop/src/kit/cardZoom.ts`.

- **S11 [K]:** Given a wheel step on both axes, when reduced, then the returned
  carries are the ones `quantizeScrollDelta` produced for each axis — a reducer
  that drops the write-back must fail.
- **S12 [K]:** Given a step that quantizes to 0 on **one** axis only, when
  reduced, then `post` is true — suppression requires *both* axes to be 0, and an
  `||` in place of the `&&` must fail.
- **S13 [K]:** Given a step that quantizes to 0 on **both** axes, when reduced,
  then `post` is false **and** the carries are still returned — a slow scroll must
  keep accumulating while nothing is posted, or it stalls forever.
- **S14 [K]:** Given distinct x and y deltas, when reduced, then each axis's step
  derives from its own delta — a reducer that swaps them must fail.
- **S15 [K]:** Given a relay instance fed repeated sub-pixel deltas, when the post
  is suppressed, then the carry is still retained, so the accumulated steps cross a
  pixel within a few events. This is the *ordering* — carry written back before the
  decision not to post — and it lives in `createScrollRelay` rather than in the
  component precisely because an ordering inside the untested React shell is
  invisible: reversing the two statements stalls a slow scroll forever with the
  whole suite green. Relay instances must not share carry state.

### Diff review
- **S8 [D] — not a test; a criterion the reviewer checks against the diff.** The
  change must touch no line of the magnify zoom path: `MAGNIFY_K` and
  `cardIframePx` in `desktop/src/kit/cardZoom.ts`, the `const MAGNIFY_TRANSFORM`
  declaration in `desktop/src/cards/HtmlCard.tsx` (cited by symbol — this change
  shifts its line), and `applyZoom` /
  `document.documentElement.style.zoom` in `desktop/src-tauri/src/card_shim.js:256-258`.
  The existing zoom-path tests must be **byte-unmodified and green**: the
  `MAGNIFY_K never upsamples` and `cardIframePx (S10)` blocks in
  `cardZoom.test.ts`, and the zoom assertions in `desktop/src/card-shim.test.ts`.
  Discharge it by quoting the `git diff --stat` plus the fact that no line in those
  four referents appears in the diff — "the suite is green" alone does not
  discharge S8, since unmodified tests pass by definition.

### Manual QA
- **S10 [Q] — the bug this spec exists to fix.** Given an HTML card in magnify
  mode on a board at a **non-integer** zoom (e.g. 1.728), when it is scrolled
  through the wheel relay continuously for at least 30 s, including direction
  reversals, then no white band appears at the top or bottom edge of the card
  body at any point. Record the build, the zoom value, and the observation in
  `desktop/qa/scroll-band-qa.md`. Also confirm scrolling still tracks the trackpad
  smoothly, including a slow one-finger drag (the S4 behaviour, observed rather
  than counted). The frame-by-frame counts in *Context* came from an ad-hoc
  screen-capture instrument that is **not** checked into the repo; this scenario
  is observational by design, and reproducing those counts is not required.
  `desktop/qa/scroll-band-probe.html`, which *is* checked in, is a different
  artifact: it reports the relayed deltas for check 1 and counts no frames.

> **Your job:** make every **[K]** scenario above pass with tests that would
> *fail if the behavior were wrong* — and discharge **[D]** and **[Q]** the way
> their entries say, since neither is dischargeable by an assertion.

- Pure logic in `desktop/src/kit/cardZoom.ts`, tested in `cardZoom.test.ts`
  (vitest) — every **[K]** scenario. The React shell has no unit tests by design
  (`docs/coding-style.md` §1.2); the `HtmlCard.tsx` change is §1.3 exception 2
  (thin wiring). Because this is spec-driven work, §1.3 discharges that exception
  with a **QA doc under `desktop/qa/`**, not a PR-body statement: write
  `desktop/qa/scroll-band-qa.md` in the shape of `desktop/qa/cull-qa.md` —
  scenario, the build it ran against, the observed result — and record S10 there.
- Follow the file's existing convention: a `describe` per behavior, with a
  comment naming the spec/issue and the *why*.
- Do not restate constants in assertions — S3 and S4 must compute the expected
  travel from the delta and the event count, not hard-code the totals.
- **Float hygiene, since every scenario here is arithmetic.** Assert
  integrality with `Number.isInteger`, never `toBe(0)` — `Math.round(-0.2)` is
  `-0`, and vitest's `toBe` is `Object.is`, so `-0` fails against `0`. Assert
  carry- and conservation-equalities against the stated `1e-9` tolerance —
  `Math.abs(a - b) < 1e-9`, or `toBeCloseTo(x, 9)` with the precision written
  out; bare `toBeCloseTo` defaults to two decimal digits and would pass on a
  whole-pixel error. `cardZoom.test.ts`
  already carries a comment recording a float trap (the `1.005` note); add the
  same for any value you had to pick around one.
- `make test` must pass (docs-check, cargo, vitest, src-tauri cargo).

## Trade-offs and Limitations

- Sub-pixel scroll precision on the relay path is quantized. The residue is
  carried, so there is no drift and no stall, but a card author observes only
  integer `scrollBy` deltas.
- The carry resets when the effect re-runs (selection, borrow, or magnify-mode
  change). The loss is bounded by one sub-pixel residue — that bound is S9, not an
  assumption — and is not observable.
- **Does not cover a card's own fractional `scrollBy`** — measured to band at
  126/200 and untouched by this change.
- **Does not cover the borrowed path**, where the iframe receives native wheel
  events directly. Unverified; the inferred mechanism is specific to programmatic
  scrolls, so native scrolling is expected to be unaffected.
