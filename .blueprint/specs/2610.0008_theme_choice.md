## Gate

**Approved 2026-10-06**, all 57 scenarios, D1 to D7 and G1 to G13, after one evaluator pass and one independent check of the values and the code claims against their sources. Implementation is in the session that wrote it.

**Changed after the gate**, by the implementation, the referee and the live run (2026-10-06, recorded in `qa/theme-choice-qa.md`):

- **`Theme.variant` is removed.** The draft said it is read from the entry. It has no reader left: the two pages ask `PageTheme`, and `ThemeSettings` compares the entry's id.
- **A theme that is already the chosen one draws its pane again.** The draft said "nothing is drawn again". The pane reads its own controls again, as it does for a tile; nothing is saved and no view takes its colours again (S53).
- **One more test of 2610.0007 is removed** than S8 listed at the gate: the one for the light scroll thumb. S10 holds its rule for all eight palettes.
- **"A ratio equal to the floor gives no finding" has no test.** The referee changed `<` to `<=` and every test stayed green. See *Trade-offs and Limitations*.
- **The two pop-ups have one width,** that of the longer title. The draft did not say.
- **The pane gives its tiles their pictures when it is made.** The window takes its size from the panes as they are made, and a tile with no picture was measured short.
- **Limits of the live run.** S33 and S52 compare with the values the record of 2610.0007 has for `f683657`, not with a build of that commit. S55 ran on a light Mac only. In S41 and S42 no frame holds a Breeze Dark fill, which is the test they name; a doc card's page is the system's dark canvas for 6 frames (S41) or 1 frame (S42) before it is the theme's page, and it is the same under Breeze Dark, so it is not from this change. S49 was not run: it is left to the user (G11).

Decisions the user made on 2026-10-06, before the draft:

- **D1.** Tarmac ships four pairs of themes: Breeze (the two of today), Solarized Light and Dark, Catppuccin Latte and Mocha, GitHub Light and Dark.
- **D2.** A theme from a file of the user is not in this change. It is #218.
- **D3.** The control is a pop-up for each appearance, under the tiles. The *Light* and *Dark* tiles draw the theme chosen for them.

- **D7.** Made at the gate: the rule "is this palette news to a page" is one tested type in `TarmacKit`, `PageTheme`, that `DocWebView` and `HTMLCardView` both use. The draft had the guard of `HTMLCardView` checked by review only, because what it guards is not on screen.

## Motivation

A user who has another palette in every other terminal cannot have it in Tarmac. 2610.0007 built what a second theme needs: one value for every colour, a change that reaches every card with no restart, and a Theme pane. It left the choice of theme out on purpose.

## Proposed Solution

### Overview

The Theme pane keeps the *Appearance* row and gains two rows: *Light theme* and *Dark theme*, each a pop-up of the themes for that appearance. The *Light* tile draws the chosen light theme and the *Dark* tile the chosen dark one. A choice for the appearance in effect applies at once to everything a theme change reaches today. Both choices are saved in `app-prefs.json`.

With nothing chosen the themes are Breeze Light and Breeze Dark, so nothing changes for a user who does nothing.

The themes are a catalogue in `TarmacKit`. What is in effect is an entry of the catalogue, not a variant.

### Key Components

- **`ThemeCatalog`** (`app/Sources/TarmacKit/ThemeCatalog.swift`, new) — the themes Tarmac has, in the order of the pop-ups, and the pure rules: which are offered for an appearance, which is the standard one, which entry an id names, and which entry is in effect.
- **`Palette`** — gains `variant` (light or dark, by the rule of `TerminalTheme.isDark`). Loses `of(_:)`. Holds the eight values.
- **`PaletteCheck`** (`app/Sources/TarmacKit/PaletteCheck.swift`, new) — the detector: the findings of a palette.
- **`ThemeVariant`** — gains `CaseIterable`, `prefsKey` and `rowTitle`.
- **`AppPrefs.Values.themes`** — the saved theme id for each appearance.
- **`ThemeCSS`** — takes a `Palette` in place of a `ThemeVariant`.
- **`PageTheme`** (`app/Sources/TarmacKit/PageTheme.swift`, new) — what a loaded page was last given, and whether a palette is news to it.
- **`DevSnapshot.Theme.name`** — the id of the theme in effect.
- **The shell** (`TarmacApp`) — `ThemeSettings` (resolves the entry in effect, saves a theme), `Theme` (holds the entry in effect), `ThemePane` (the two pop-ups), `ThemeTile` (draws a given palette), `DocWebView` and `HTMLCardView` (their guards).
- **`scripts/qa/settings-window.swift`** — one verb for the pop-ups; `rows` prints them.

`TarmacTerm` does not change.

### Data Flow

Launch:

1. `AppPrefsStore` loads `AppPrefs.Values`, with `theme` and `themes`.
2. `ThemeSettings` sets `NSApp.appearance` from the choice, as today, and reads whether the Mac is dark.
3. `ThemeCatalog.inEffect(choice:themes:systemIsDark:)` gives the entry. `Theme` holds it. `Theme.palette` is the entry's palette.
4. Every view takes its colours from `Theme.palette`, as today.

A theme chosen in a pop-up:

1. The pop-up of an appearance reports an id. An id equal to the one chosen for that appearance (the saved one, or the standard one) does nothing: nothing is saved and no view takes its colours again.
2. `ThemeSettings` saves it through `AppPrefsStore`.
3. The tile of that appearance, and the *Auto* tile, draw their pictures again.
4. `ThemeSettings` resolves the entry in effect. **If the entry's id changed**, it sets the entry on `Theme` and calls `onChange`. The walk of 2610.0007 then runs, with the same reach.
5. A doc card gives its page the new declarations, and an HTML card its new `--bg1`, when the **palette** differs from the one the page was last given.

A tile pressed, and a change of the Mac's appearance under *Auto*: as in 2610.0007, with step 4 above in place of "if the variant changed".

### Interface Contract

**The catalogue.**

```swift
public enum ThemeCatalog {
    public struct Entry: Equatable, Sendable {
        public let id: String          // what the file and the snapshot hold
        public let title: String       // what a pop-up shows
        public let palette: Palette
        public var variant: ThemeVariant { palette.variant }
    }

    /// Every theme, in the order of the pop-ups.
    public static let all: [Entry]
    /// The entries of `all` with that variant, in the same order.
    public static func offered(for variant: ThemeVariant) -> [Entry]
    public static func standard(for variant: ThemeVariant) -> Entry
    /// The offered entry with that id, or the standard one.
    public static func entry(_ id: String?, for variant: ThemeVariant) -> Entry
    public static func inEffect(
        choice: ThemeChoice, themes: [ThemeVariant: String], systemIsDark: Bool
    ) -> Entry
}
```

| id | title | variant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `breeze-light` | Breeze Light | light |
| `breeze-dark` | Breeze Dark | dark |
| `catppuccin-latte` | Catppuccin Latte | light |
| `catppuccin-mocha` | Catppuccin Mocha | dark |
| `github-light` | GitHub Light | light |
| `github-dark` | GitHub Dark | dark |
| `solarized-light` | Solarized Light | light |
| `solarized-dark` | Solarized Dark | dark |

- `all` is the eight rows in that order.
- `standard(for: .light)` is `breeze-light` and `standard(for: .dark)` is `breeze-dark`.
- `entry(id, for: variant)` compares the id exactly (`"GitHub-Light"` is not one). `nil`, an id that no entry has, and the id of an entry of the other variant all give the standard entry of `variant`.
- `inEffect` is `entry(themes[v], for: v)` where `v` is `choice.inEffect(systemIsDark:)`.

```swift
extension Palette {
    /// `.dark` when the terminal background's WCAG relative luminance is less
    /// than the terminal foreground's; `.light` otherwise, also when equal.
    public var variant: ThemeVariant { get }
}

public enum ThemeVariant: String, CaseIterable, Sendable {
    case light, dark                        // light first
    public var prefsKey: String             // "theme_light", "theme_dark"
    public var rowTitle: String             // "Light theme", "Dark theme"
}
```

`Palette.variant` is the rule of `TerminalTheme.isDark`, so a theme is offered for the appearance its terminal reports to a program. `Palette.of(_:)` does not exist after this change.

`Palette` and `Palette.Terminal` keep the memberwise initializers they have today. They are internal. A test that needs a palette with a changed member (S11 to S16) builds it with them, through `@testable import TarmacKit`, as every suite of `TarmacKitTests` imports the module today.

**The file.** `app-prefs.json` gains two keys.

| Key | Type | Absent means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `theme_light` | string: the id of a theme offered for the light appearance | `"breeze-light"` |
| `theme_dark` | string: the id of a theme offered for the dark appearance | `"breeze-dark"` |

```swift
extension AppPrefs.Values {
    /// The theme chosen for an appearance; no entry is the standard one.
    public var themes: [ThemeVariant: String]       // default [:]
}
```

- `themes` is a stored member of `Values`, and `Values.init` gains `themes: [ThemeVariant: String] = [:]` as its last parameter.
- Decode: a key gives an entry of `themes` only when its value is a JSON string equal to the id of a theme offered for that appearance, and that theme is not the standard one. Anything else gives no entry: no key, a number, an id no theme has, the id of a theme of the other appearance, the standard id. Each key is read on its own. The rules for a damaged file do not change: the whole file reads as `Values()`.
- Encode: `,"theme_light":"<id>"` and then `,"theme_dark":"<id>"`, after `theme`. A key is written only when `themes` holds, for that appearance, the id of an offered theme that is not the standard one. So the bytes for every `Values` that exists today do not change.
- `decode(encode(v)) == v` holds for every `v` whose `themes` entries are ids that are written.
- Reading never writes the file. A name that gives no entry stays in the file until a preference is saved (G9).
- `AppPrefsStrictnessTests` and the cases of `AppPrefsTests` that exist pass unedited.

How a proposal is made:

- *X with n % of Y* is, for each channel of sRGB, `X × (1 − n/100) + Y × n/100`, rounded to the nearest whole number. A value that is exactly a half goes to the even number: four values of the tables are such a half.
- *tuned to f* is a rule of the same kind as the one 2610.0007 used for Breeze Light, and is new here: keep the hue and the saturation of the upstream value (HSL), and move its lightness in steps of 0.5 % away from the fills (darker in a light theme, lighter in a dark one) until the colour has a contrast of `f` or more on each of `bg0`, `bg1` and `bg2`. `text` and `muted` are tuned to 4.5; `agent`, `amber`, `ok`, `consoleError` and the repo colours to 3. A value that already passes is not changed.

The tables are the contract for the values. The rule only says where they came from.

**The detector.**

```swift
public enum PaletteCheck {
    public struct Finding: Equatable, Sendable {
        public enum Part: Sendable { case terminal, chrome }
        public let part: Part
        public let subject: String     // "ansi 15", "foreground", "muted", "agent"
        public let ground: String      // "background", "bg0", "bg1", "bg2"
        public let ratio: Double       // `Contrast.ratio` of the two
        public let floor: Double
    }

    /// Every pair of the palette that has less contrast than its floor.
    public static func findings(_ palette: Palette) -> [Finding]
}
```

The rules, in the order the findings come:

| Rule | Part | Subject | Ground | Floor |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | terminal | `ansi 0` … `ansi 15`, by index | `background` | 3 |
| R2 | terminal | `foreground` | `background` | 7 |
| R3 | chrome | `text`, `muted`, `agent`, `amber`, `ok`, `consoleError`, in that order | `bg0`, then `bg1`, then `bg2` | 4.5 for `text` and `muted`, 3 for the other four |

- A pair gives a finding when its ratio is less than its floor. A ratio equal to the floor gives none.
- R3 goes fill by fill: every subject on `bg0`, then every subject on `bg1`, then on `bg2`.
- The rules are the same for a light palette and a dark one (G7).
- `findings` reads nothing but the palette. It refuses nothing and changes nothing.

## Alternatives Considered

### Tune every theme to the terminal floors

Rejected by D5. Solarized Light would change 7 ANSI colours and its foreground, and Catppuccin Latte 9. The themes would then differ from the user's other terminals, which is the problem #217 is for.

### One key that carries the appearance and both themes

Ghostty writes `theme = light:X,dark:Y`. Rejected by D4: the parser would have to accept the plain values that `f683657` writes too, and one bad half would make the reader decide what to do with the other half. Three keys are each read on their own, as every key of the file is.

### A grid of preview tiles for each appearance

Rejected by D3. It needs more room than the window has, and the two tiles that exist already show the chosen themes.

### A closed `enum` of themes

`enum ThemeID { case breezeLight, … }` is shorter. The catalogue is a list of values because #218 adds entries that are not known when the app is built. This change does not build for #218; it only does not close the door.

### Keep `Palette.of(_:)` for the tests

Then `ThemeCSS.properties(.dark)` and a tile drawn from `Palette.of(.dark)` still compile, and give Breeze whatever theme is chosen. With the function removed, the compiler finds each such caller (G10).

### The upstream selection colours

A Ghostty file gives `selection-background` and `selection-foreground`, to be used together and opaque. `Palette.Terminal` holds one selection colour with an alpha, drawn over the cells. Solarized Light's `eee8d5` at 0.3 on `fdf6e3` cannot be seen. A second selection model is a change to `TarmacTerm`'s renderer, and is not in this change (G5).

## Migration & Rollback

No migration. A file written before this change has no `theme_light` and no `theme_dark`, and reads as Breeze. Release 0.16.0 (`289d208`) has no theme setting: the key `theme` of 2610.0007 is on `main` and is not released yet. So a file this change wrote, read by 0.16.0, reads as before there was a theme, and loses `theme`, `theme_light` and `theme_dark` at 0.16.0's next save.

## Security Considerations

A theme id from the file is compared with the ids of the catalogue and is never put into a page, a script or a path. The declarations a page gets are built from `UInt32` colours, and are passed to a loaded page as arguments, as today.

## For the Implementing Agent

> **Your job:** make every acceptance scenario above pass with tests that would *fail if the behavior were wrong*. A green suite that passes for the wrong reason does not satisfy this contract — `/verify` will hunt for vacuous tests by asking, of each behavior, "what is the smallest change that breaks this, and would any test catch it?"

Implement however you work best. Only the result is checked. The repository's own rules still bind (`docs/coding-style.md`): red first and observed red; the smallest correct diff; no line-noise comments; do not run `cargo fmt`; verify with `make`, not the editor.

Points that are easy to get wrong here:

- **Three guards compare variants.** `ThemeSettings.resolve` returns when the variant is the same, `DocWebView` when `themeGiven` is the same variant, `HTMLCardView` when `backdropGiven` is. Breeze Dark to Catppuccin Mocha passes none of them. `ThemeSettings` must compare the entry's id, and each of the two views must ask its `PageTheme` (S57). S35 is the live check for `ThemeSettings`, and S35 and S39 for `DocWebView`; S35 is written as dark to dark on purpose. The host page's backdrop of an HTML card is not on screen (2610.0007, *Open Questions*), so `HTMLCardView` has no live check: it is right when it uses `PageTheme` as `DocWebView` does.
- **`Palette.of` has four callers** at `f683657`: `Theme.palette`, `ThemeCSS` (twice) and `ThemeTile`. A tile drawn from the standard palette passes every test and fails S44.
- **A tile's images are made once,** in `ThemeTile.button`, as `image` and `alternateImage`. A chosen theme must make both again for its tile and for *Auto*.
- **A theme for the appearance not in effect must not call `onChange`** (S44), and must still be saved.
- **`onChange` is for a change of the entry; the file is written for a change of what is chosen.** *Auto* to *Dark* on a dark Mac changes the file and no colour.
- **The values are the tables.** Do not compute a tuned value or a mix in the code: write the literal, as the two Breeze palettes are written. The tune rule's steps are in HSL and a second implementation can land one step away.
- **`Palette.variant` and `TerminalTheme.isDark` are one rule in two modules,** because `TarmacTerm` does not depend on `TarmacKit`. Equal luminance is light in both.
- **Upstream ANSI values are not to be "fixed".** ANSI 15 of Solarized Light is its background. S19 holds the findings to the table, so a tuned terminal colour fails it.
- **`findings` uses `<`, not `<=`.** Five chrome values are within 0.03 of their floor (`agent` on `bg2` of Catppuccin Latte is 3.0095, the nearest). S13 and S14 hold the two sides of 7 and of 4.5. No pair of 8-bit colours was found exactly on a floor, so no test tells `<` from `<=` (see *Trade-offs and Limitations*).
- **`AppPrefs.encode` writes a literal.** An id is written only after it is compared with the catalogue, so no escaping is needed and none must be added by hand.
- **`AppPrefsTests.testTheThemeIsWrittenLast` must stay green unedited:** with no themes chosen, `theme` is still the last member.
- **`DevSnapshotTests` pins the compact bytes of the snapshot, and the test of 2610.0007 S18 pins `theme` to two members.** Those two tests change, and the suite's `input` helper gives a name; the test of the top-level keys does not change.
- **The pop-ups must be real `NSPopUpButton`s** the Accessibility API can read and set, after the tiles in the order of the controls. The font QA verbs count controls of the Fonts pane only; `theme` finds the tiles by role. Check that `rows`, `pick` and `theme` still work.
- **The window has one size.** Two more rows must fit in it. If they do not, the window's one size grows for both panes; S33 then records the new frame and says so.
- **Never launch `dist/Tarmac.app`** and never kill a daemon by name. All live QA is against `make run` on a fresh channel (`make run DEV_SOCKET=… DEV_ENV=…`); stop its daemon with `make kill-daemon`.
- **Do not change the Mac's appearance** without the user's leave (S49). Every other QA scenario is written to run with a tile pressed.

Test principles, the ones `/verify` scores against:

- **Behavioral over structural** — assert observable output, not internals.
- **Every test can fail** — S9 compares with the tables of this spec, which is the contract for the values; S17 is the test that fails when a chrome token of a new theme is too faint; S12 to S16 build a palette that breaks one rule and name the finding.
- **Deterministic, isolated, readable** — temp directories per test, no shared state, no dependence on the Mac's appearance: `systemIsDark` is an argument.

- [ ] `make test` is green, and `make bundle` succeeds.
- [ ] Every acceptance scenario S1…S57 maps to a test, a command, or a recorded QA run in `qa/theme-choice-qa.md`.

## Trade-offs and Limitations

- **Some terminal text cannot be read in some themes.** The findings table is the list. ANSI 15 of Solarized Light is its background, and ANSI 0 of every dark theme is near its background. This is the same in every terminal that uses these palettes, and is D5.
- **Catppuccin differs from Ghostty 1.3.1** (G2): Latte in ANSI 0 and 7 to 15, Mocha in ANSI 7 and 9 to 15. The values are those of the collection at the pinned commit.
- **A tuned mark is not the upstream accent.** `agent` of Catppuccin Latte is `148187`, not the teal `179299`: the upstream value has less than 3 on `bg2`. The terminal's ANSI 6 stays `179299`.
- **The fills of the two Solarized themes are mostly proposals.** Solarized has two background tones for each mode.
- **`agent` is not the brand accent** of GitHub or Catppuccin (G4).
- **Terminal colours 16 to 255, the scroll thumb, the shadows, the quit notice and the menus of the menu bar** are as 2610.0007 left them, in every theme.
- **An HTML card follows light or dark, not the theme.** A document that follows gets the system's canvas (`1e1e1e` or `ffffff`) under every theme.
- **The host page of an HTML card has no live check after a change.** Its `--bg1` is not on screen, as in 2610.0007. The rule it uses is tested (S57); that `HTMLCardView` uses it is wiring.
- **The detector has no caller but the tests** until #218.
- **"A ratio equal to the floor gives no finding" has no test.** The referee's change of `<` to `<=` left every test green. Its search found no grey pair, and no pair of the eight palettes, with a ratio of exactly 3, 4.5 or 7, so for 8-bit colours the two are most likely the same rule. This was not proved.
- **A theme has no name in another language,** and the pop-ups have no preview but the tile.
- **`Palette.variant` and `TerminalTheme.isDark` are the same rule written twice** (see *For the Implementing Agent*).
