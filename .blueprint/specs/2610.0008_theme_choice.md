## Motivation

A user who has another palette in every other terminal cannot have it in Tarmac. 2610.0007 built what a second theme needs: one value for every colour, a change that reaches every card with no restart, and a Theme pane. It left the choice of theme out on purpose.

Revision 1 gave the choice. Three things were wrong with its form:

- A pop-up shows a name and no colour. To see a theme, the user had to choose it, and a choice changes the whole app.
- A user who wants one theme at all hours could not have it: a dark theme could not be chosen for the light appearance.
- With a dark theme for the light appearance, the parts that the system draws would be light on a dark app, if the app's appearance still came from the choice.

## Proposed Solution

### Overview

The Theme pane keeps the *Appearance* row with its three tiles. Under it, one grouped box has two columns:

- **The list**, on the left: one row for each theme of the catalogue, with a small swatch, the theme's title, and a mark *Light* or *Dark* (or both) when the theme is the one chosen for that appearance.
- **The showcase**, on the right: the theme that is selected in the list, as a title, the words "light theme" or "dark theme", a picture of a board drawn in the theme's colours, two checkboxes (*Apply to Light*, *Apply to Dark*) and one line about contrast.

The user looks first and applies second. A selected row changes the showcase only. A checkbox chooses the shown theme for an appearance. A choice for the appearance in effect applies at once to everything a theme change reaches today. Both choices are saved in `app-prefs.json`.

Any theme can be chosen for either appearance, and one theme for both. The *Light* tile draws the theme chosen for the light appearance, the *Dark* tile the one chosen for the dark appearance, and *Auto* half of each.

The app's appearance is the variant of the theme in effect. So the Settings window, a menu opened in a window, and what a page sees as `prefers-color-scheme` follow the theme, also when the choice is *Light* and its theme is a dark one. A menu of the menu bar does not: it takes the Mac's appearance, as today.

With nothing chosen the themes are Breeze Light and Breeze Dark, so nothing changes for a user who does nothing.

The themes are a catalogue in `TarmacKit`. What is in effect is an entry of the catalogue, not a variant.

- **`ThemeVariant`** — `CaseIterable`, `prefsKey`. Revision 2: `title` replaces `rowTitle`.

- **`ThemeCSS`**, **`PageTheme`** (`app/Sources/TarmacKit/PageTheme.swift`), **`DevSnapshot.Theme.name`** — not changed by revision 2.
- **`ThemeBrowser`** (`app/Sources/TarmacKit/ThemeBrowser.swift`, new in revision 2, tests in `ThemeBrowserTests`) — the pure rules of the list and the showcase: the state of a box, the theme a box gives, the marks of a row, the contrast note, and the fixed words.

### Data Flow

Launch:

1. `AppPrefsStore` loads `AppPrefs.Values`, with `theme` and `themes`.
2. `ThemeSettings` reads the preference `AppleInterfaceStyle`, and `ThemeChoice.systemIsDark(interfaceStyle:)` gives whether the Mac is dark.
3. `ThemeCatalog.inEffect(choice:themes:systemIsDark:)` gives the entry. `Theme` holds it. `Theme.palette` is the entry's palette.
4. `ThemeSettings` sets `NSApp.appearance` from the entry's variant. Steps 2 to 4 are done before the first view is made.
5. Every view takes its colours from `Theme.palette`, as today.

The Settings window opens:

1. The shown theme of the pane is the theme in effect. The list selects its row.
2. The pane draws its tiles, the marks of each row (`ThemeBrowser.marks`), and the showcase of the shown theme.

A row selected in the list:

1. `ThemePane` keeps that row's theme as the shown theme.
2. The showcase draws it: the title, the caption, the picture, the two boxes (`ThemeBrowser.box`) and the note (`ThemeBrowser.contrastNote`).
3. Nothing more. `ThemeSettings` is not called, nothing is saved, the snapshot does not change, and no view of a board takes its colours again.

A box set or cleared:

1. `ThemePane` asks `ThemeBrowser.toggled(shown:on:for:)` for the theme that the appearance has now, and gives it to `ThemeSettings`.
2. `ThemeSettings` saves it through `AppPrefsStore`. A theme equal to the one chosen for that appearance does nothing: nothing is saved and no view takes its colours again. A box cannot give such a theme.
3. The pane draws its marks, its boxes, the tile of that appearance and the *Auto* tile again.
4. `ThemeSettings` resolves the entry in effect. **If the entry's id changed**, it sets the entry on `Theme`, sets `NSApp.appearance` from the entry's variant, and calls `onChange`. The walk of 2610.0007 then runs, with the same reach.
5. A doc card gives its page the new declarations, and an HTML card its new `--bg1`, when the **palette** differs from the one the page was last given.

A tile pressed: `ThemeSettings` saves the choice, and step 4 runs. The marks and the shown theme do not change.

A change of the Mac's appearance: the observer of the preference `AppleInterfaceStyle` reads the preference again, and step 4 runs. Under *Light* and under *Dark* the entry is the same one, so nothing changes. Nothing is saved.

### Interface Contract

**The catalogue.**

```swift
public enum ThemeCatalog {
    public struct Entry: Equatable, Sendable {
        public let id: String          // what the file and the snapshot hold
        public let title: String       // what the list and the showcase show
        public let palette: Palette
        public var variant: ThemeVariant { palette.variant }
    }

    /// Every theme, in the order of the list.
    public static let all: [Entry]
    public static func standard(for variant: ThemeVariant) -> Entry
    /// The entry of `all` with that id, whatever its variant, or the
    /// standard one of `variant`.
    public static func entry(_ id: String?, for variant: ThemeVariant) -> Entry
    public static func inEffect(
        choice: ThemeChoice, themes: [ThemeVariant: String], systemIsDark: Bool
    ) -> Entry
}
```

| id | title | variant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `breeze-light` | Breeze Light | light |
| `breeze-dark` | Breeze Dark | dark |
| `catppuccin-latte` | Catppuccin Latte | light |
| `catppuccin-mocha` | Catppuccin Mocha | dark |

- `all` is the eight rows in that order.
- `standard(for: .light)` is `breeze-light` and `standard(for: .dark)` is `breeze-dark`.
- `entry(id, for: variant)` compares the id exactly (`"GitHub-Light"` is not one). The id of an entry of `all` gives that entry for both variants: `entry("catppuccin-mocha", for: .light)` is Catppuccin Mocha. `nil` and an id that no entry has give the standard entry of `variant`.

- `ThemeCatalog.offered(for:)` does not exist after revision 2 (D9).

The column *variant* says what a theme is. It no longer says where a theme can be chosen. Four things read it: the app's appearance (D10), the `color-scheme` of the doc page, what a terminal reports to a program, and the caption of the showcase.

**The file.** `app-prefs.json` has two keys for the themes.

| Key | Type | Absent means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `theme_light` | string: the id of a theme of the catalogue that is not `breeze-light` | `"breeze-light"` |
| `theme_dark` | string: the id of a theme of the catalogue that is not `breeze-dark` | `"breeze-dark"` |

```swift
extension AppPrefs.Values {
    /// The theme chosen for an appearance; no entry is the standard one.
    public var themes: [ThemeVariant: String]       // default [:]
}
```

- `themes` is a stored member of `Values`, and `Values.init` has `themes: [ThemeVariant: String] = [:]` as its last parameter.
- Decode: a key gives an entry of `themes` only when its value is a JSON string equal to the id of an entry of `ThemeCatalog.all`, and that entry is not the standard theme of the key's appearance. The variant of the theme does not matter (D9). So `"theme_light":"catppuccin-mocha"` and `"theme_light":"breeze-dark"` each give an entry. Anything else gives no entry: no key, a number, `null`, an array, an id no theme has (`"sepia"`), an id in another letter case (`"GitHub-Light"`), and the standard id of that appearance (`"theme_light":"breeze-light"`). Each key is read on its own. The rules for a damaged file do not change: the whole file reads as `Values()`.
- Encode: `,"theme_light":"<id>"` and then `,"theme_dark":"<id>"`, after `theme`. A key is written only when `themes` holds, for that appearance, an id that decode gives an entry for. So the bytes for every `Values` that exists on `f683657` do not change.
- `decode(encode(v)) == v` holds for every `v` whose `themes` entries are ids that are written.
- Reading never writes the file. A name that gives no entry stays in the file until a preference is saved (G9).
- `AppPrefsStrictnessTests` and the cases of `AppPrefsTests` that are on `f683657` pass unedited.

How a proposal is made:

- *X with n % of Y* is, for each channel of sRGB, `X × (1 − n/100) + Y × n/100`, rounded to the nearest whole number. A value that is exactly a half goes to the even number: four values of the tables are such a half.
- *tuned to f* is a rule of the same kind as the one 2610.0007 used for Breeze Light, and is new here: keep the hue and the saturation of the upstream value (HSL), and move its lightness in steps of 0.5 % away from the fills (darker in a light theme, lighter in a dark one) until the colour has a contrast of `f` or more on each of `bg0`, `bg1` and `bg2`. `text` and `muted` are tuned to 4.5; `agent`, `amber`, `ok`, `consoleError` and the repo colours to 3. A value that already passes is not changed.

The tables are the contract for the values. The rule only says where they came from.

  | `shown` is `chosen` | `shown` is the standard theme of `variant` | `isOn` | `isEnabled` |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | yes | no | true | true |
  | yes | yes | true | false |
  | no | no | false | true |
  | no | yes | false | true |

  "The standard theme of `variant`" is `breeze-light` for `.light` and `breeze-dark` for `.dark`. Breeze Dark chosen for the light appearance is not the standard theme of that appearance, so its *Apply to Light* box is on and enabled.
- `toggled`: with `on` true it gives `shown`. With `on` false it gives `ThemeCatalog.standard(for: variant)`, whatever the variant of `shown` is.
- `marks`: the variants `v`, in the order `.light`, `.dark`, for which `ThemeCatalog.entry(themes[v], for: v)` has the id of `entry`. `themes` is the member of `AppPrefs.Values`, where no entry is the standard theme. - `contrastNote`: with `n` the number of findings of `PaletteCheck.findings(palette)` whose part is `terminal`:

  | `n` | The line |
  | --- | --- |
  | 0 | `Every terminal colour passes the contrast floors.` |
  | 1 | `1 terminal colour has low contrast on the background.` |
  | 2 or more | `<n> terminal colours have low contrast on the background.` |

  A finding whose part is `chrome` is not counted (G14). A finding for the `foreground` is counted: its part is `terminal`. The note warns. It blocks nothing, and it disables no box.
- `boxTitle` is `"Apply to "` and then `variant.title`.
- `caption` is the raw value of `entry.variant` and then `" theme"`.

## Alternatives Considered

### Tune every theme to the terminal floors

Rejected by D5. Solarized Light would change 7 ANSI colours and its foreground, and Catppuccin Latte 9. The themes would then differ from the user's other terminals, which is the problem #217 is for.

### One key that carries the appearance and both themes

Ghostty writes `theme = light:X,dark:Y`. Rejected by D4: the parser would have to accept the plain values that `f683657` writes too, and one bad half would make the reader decide what to do with the other half. Three keys are each read on their own, as every key of the file is.

### Two pop-ups, one for each appearance

This is revision 1 (D3), and it is what `c6229ea` has. Replaced by D8. A pop-up shows the names of the themes and no colour: the only way to see a theme was to choose it, and a choice changes every card. Its two menus also were the light-only rule: each one listed the themes of one variant. With D9 each menu would list all eight themes, and the pane would still have no preview. One list with no sections holds D9 with no more parts.

### Keep the light-only rule, with disabled boxes

The first mock of the pane kept the rule of revision 1: a box was disabled for a theme of the other variant. Replaced by D9. With that rule a user who wants one theme under both appearances cannot have it. With D9 a box is disabled in one case only (the standard theme of an appearance while it is chosen for it), and the pane needs no sections.

### An appearance on each window, and none on the app

`NSApp.appearance` stays `nil`, `NSApp.effectiveAppearance` still reports the Mac, and each window gets the appearance of the theme in effect. This keeps the read and the observer that `c6229ea` has. It was measured (*Measured on 2026-10-06, for D10*), and it is not the contract because of what stayed light on a light Mac with a dark window: a second window with no appearance of its own, an alert shown as a window of its own or with `runModal`, a child window, and a menu opened with no view. A tooltip was not shown to be dark. Each new window would need a line of code, and a window that the code forgets follows the Mac, not the theme. With the appearance on the app, all of these were dark with nothing set on them. The cost of D10 is the read of the preference `AppleInterfaceStyle`, which Apple does not document.

### A grid of preview tiles for each appearance

Rejected by D3 in revision 1: it needs more room than the window had, and the two tiles that exist already show the chosen themes. D8 gives the preview in another form: one large showcase for the row that is selected. A grid shows eight small pictures, and more after #218.

### A closed `enum` of themes

`enum ThemeID { case breezeLight, … }` is shorter. The catalogue is a list of values because #218 adds entries that are not known when the app is built. This change does not build for #218; it only does not close the door.

### Keep `Palette.of(_:)` for the tests

Then `ThemeCSS.properties(.dark)` and a tile drawn from `Palette.of(.dark)` still compile, and give Breeze whatever theme is chosen. With the function removed, the compiler finds each such caller (G10).

### The upstream selection colours

A Ghostty file gives `selection-background` and `selection-foreground`, to be used together and opaque. `Palette.Terminal` holds one selection colour with an alpha, drawn over the cells. Solarized Light's `eee8d5` at 0.3 on `fdf6e3` cannot be seen. A second selection model is a change to `TarmacTerm`'s renderer, and is not in this change (G5).

## Migration & Rollback

No migration. A file written before this change has no `theme_light` and no `theme_dark`, and reads as Breeze. Release 0.16.0 (`289d208`) has no theme setting: the key `theme` of 2610.0007 is on `main` and is not released yet. So a file this change wrote, read by 0.16.0, reads as before there was a theme, and loses `theme`, `theme_light` and `theme_dark` at 0.16.0's next save.

Revision 1 to revision 2: `c6229ea` is not released. A file that `c6229ea` wrote holds, at most, the id of a light theme that is not Breeze Light for `theme_light` and the id of a dark theme that is not Breeze Dark for `theme_dark`. Revision 2 reads each of them as `c6229ea` does, with no change. Nothing was released with the light-only rule, so no released build reads a file of revision 2 with that rule. A build of `c6229ea` that reads a file of revision 2 with, for example, `"theme_light":"catppuccin-mocha"` reads Breeze Light, and leaves the key out at its next save.

## Security Considerations

A theme id from the file is compared with the ids of the catalogue and is never put into a page, a script or a path. The declarations a page gets are built from `UInt32` colours, and are passed to a loaded page as arguments, as today.

The value of the preference `AppleInterfaceStyle` is compared with one literal. It is not written, and it is not put into a page, a script or a path.

## For the Implementing Agent

> **Your job:** make every acceptance scenario above pass with tests that would *fail if the behavior were wrong*. A green suite that passes for the wrong reason does not satisfy this contract — `/verify` will hunt for vacuous tests by asking, of each behavior, "what is the smallest change that breaks this, and would any test catch it?"

Implement however you work best. Only the result is checked. The repository's own rules still bind (`docs/coding-style.md`): red first and observed red; the smallest correct diff; no line-noise comments; do not run `cargo fmt`; verify with `make`, not the editor.

The tree at `c6229ea` passes revision 1. Revision 2 is a change on that tree. For each rewritten or new [auto] scenario, see its test fail on `c6229ea` first. Two checks cannot fail there, and each says so: S30 and the last check of S64. They guard a rule that `c6229ea` already holds.

Points of revision 2 that are easy to get wrong:

- **Tests of revision 1 hold the old rule.** Rewrite them first and see them fail: the tests of S2 and S4 in `ThemeCatalogTests`; the test of S6 in `ThemeChoiceTests` (`rowTitle`); in `AppPrefsTests` the tests of S22 and S24, and the test of S25, which builds its values from `offered(for:)`.
- **`standard(for:)` is made from `offered(for:)` today.** With `offered` removed it must still give `breeze-light` and `breeze-dark` (S3).
- **The file's rule follows `entry`.** `ThemeCatalog.saved` (`AppPrefs.written` when this was written) keeps an id that a theme has and that is not the standard one. With the new `entry` it may need no change. S21, S22 and S24 are the check: `breeze-dark` for Light is written, `breeze-light` for Light is not.
- **`NSApp.appearance` at launch.** `Theme.entry` starts as Breeze Dark, and `resolve()` returns when the id is the same. On a fresh channel the id is the same, so a line that sets the appearance only after that guard never runs: the app then has no appearance, and on a light Mac the Settings window is light under Breeze Dark. Set the appearance before the first view in every case (S55, first state).
- **`resolve()` must set `NSApp.appearance` at every change of the entry.** Today a chosen theme calls `resolve()`, and only the launch and a changed choice call `apply()`, which sets the appearance. The set must be before the early return of `resolve()`: on a fresh channel `Theme.entry` is already Breeze Dark, the return is taken, and a set after it never runs, so the app would follow the Mac. A box that puts a dark theme in effect under the choice *Light* changes the theme and not the choice (S66).
- **The appearance is from the entry's variant, never from the choice.** A line `case .auto: nil` left in place passes every [auto] test and fails S49 and S55.
- **Do not read `NSApp.effectiveAppearance`.** With an appearance set on the app it reports that appearance. An app that reads it under *Auto* reads its own last answer and never follows the Mac again. S64 is the grep; S49 is the live check.
- **The observer of the preference.** `ThemeSettings` is not an `NSObject`. The typed `observe(_:)` needs a key path, and `UserDefaults` has none for this key unless an `@objc dynamic` property with the key's exact name is declared. The spike measured the other form: an `NSObject` observer added with `addObserver(_:forKeyPath:options:context:)`. Keep the observer for the life of `ThemeSettings`, as `appearanceWatch` is kept today. If `ThemeSettings` can end before the app does, remove the observer then. The callback today uses `MainActor.assumeIsolated`, which stops the app when it is called off the main thread. The spike saw four deliveries, all on the main thread. That is not a proof for every delivery. So the callback of the new observer must not assume its thread: it goes to the main thread first, and only there reads the preference and calls `resolve()`.
- **The order at launch.** The palette and the app's appearance are both set before the first view is made, as the palette is today (`AppDelegate` makes `ThemeSettings` before the window).
- **A selected row saves nothing.** The table reports a selection that the code made too (at the window's opening). The handler must only set the shown theme. S53 is the check.
- **The shown theme is the pane's state, not `ThemeSettings`'.** `SettingsWindowController.show` reloads the panes only when the window is not visible: that is the place where the shown theme becomes the theme in effect (S71).
- **A box must draw the tiles again,** for its appearance and for *Auto*, as a pop-up did (S44, S66, S67).
- **A cleared box gives the standard theme of the box's appearance,** not of the shown theme's variant (S59).
- **The picture and the swatch use the palette only.** A system colour in them changes with the app's appearance. S65 shows a light theme while the app is dark, and reads the same points again under a light app. The picture must be an element that the script can find, with a frame (`picture frame=…`): S65 gives its points from that frame.
- **The window takes its one size when it is made,** from the fitting size of the panes. A picture with no size of its own is measured as nothing (the same trap as the tiles, *Changed after the gate*). The Fonts pane must still be whole in the larger window (S33).
- **Two tables.** The sidebar and the list are both tables, and the script reads rows by role. `pane` and the `pane=` line of `rows` must read the sidebar's rows only, and `show` the list's rows only (S70). An accessibility identifier on each table is one way.
- **Checkboxes and tiles.** A tile is a radio button that sets its own role, because a radio button with an image reports the role of a checkbox (`ThemeTile`). Find a box by its title, not by its role alone.
- **`rows` prints every static text of the window** in its `label` and `labels=` lines. The list and the showcase add many. Check that `theme`, `pick`, `size`, `step` and `focus` still find their controls (S56).
- **The Mac's appearance is changed in S49 and S69 only,** with System Events, and is set back and checked. No other scenario changes it.

Points of revision 1 that still hold. `c6229ea` has them right:

- **The three guards compare the entry or the palette, not the variant.** `ThemeSettings` compares the entry's id, and `DocWebView` and `HTMLCardView` each ask a `PageTheme` (S57). S35 is the live check for `ThemeSettings`, and S35 and S39 for `DocWebView`; S35 is written as dark to dark on purpose. The host page's backdrop of an HTML card is not on screen (2610.0007, *Open Questions*), so `HTMLCardView` has no live check.
- **A tile's images are made once for each state,** as `image` and `alternateImage`. A chosen theme must make both again for its tile and for *Auto*.
- **A theme for the appearance not in effect must not call `onChange`** (S44), and must still be saved.
- **`onChange` is for a change of the entry; the file is written for a change of what is chosen.** *Auto* to *Dark* on a dark Mac changes the file and no colour.
- **The values are the tables.** Do not compute a tuned value or a mix in the code: write the literal, as the two Breeze palettes are written. Revision 2 changes no colour value.
- **`Palette.variant` and `TerminalTheme.isDark` are one rule in two modules,** because `TarmacTerm` does not depend on `TarmacKit`. Equal luminance is light in both.
- **Upstream ANSI values are not to be "fixed".** ANSI 15 of Solarized Light is its background. S19 holds the findings to the table, so a tuned terminal colour fails it. The contrast note reports such a colour; it is not a reason to change one.
- **`findings` uses `<`, not `<=`.** Five chrome values are within 0.03 of their floor (`agent` on `bg2` of Catppuccin Latte is 3.0095, the nearest). S13 and S14 hold the two sides of 7 and of 4.5. No pair of 8-bit colours was found exactly on a floor, so no test tells `<` from `<=` (see *Trade-offs and Limitations*).

- **`AppPrefsTests.testTheThemeIsWrittenLast` must stay green unedited:** with no themes chosen, `theme` is still the last member.
- **Never launch `dist/Tarmac.app`** and never kill a daemon by name. All live QA is against `make run` on a fresh channel (`make run DEV_SOCKET=… DEV_ENV=…`); stop its daemon with `make kill-daemon`.

Test principles, the ones `/verify` scores against:

- **Behavioral over structural** — assert observable output, not internals.
- **Every test can fail** — S9 compares with the tables of this spec, which is the contract for the values; S17 is the test that fails when a chrome token of a new theme is too faint; S12 to S16 build a palette that breaks one rule and name the finding; S58 to S63 each name the wrong implementation that they fail.
- **Deterministic, isolated, readable** — temp directories per test, no shared state, no dependence on the Mac's appearance: `systemIsDark` and `interfaceStyle` are arguments.

## Trade-offs and Limitations

- **A system colour read outside a draw pass is still the light one.** With the app's appearance forced dark on a light Mac, the spike read `NSAppearance.currentDrawing()` as `aqua` and `NSColor.labelColor` as `000000` outside `draw`, also in `viewDidChangeEffectiveAppearance`. No code at `c6229ea` reads a system colour that way: the tiles, the Fonts pane and `ChromeLabel` resolve theirs when they draw. New code must do the same.
- **A row's swatch is not sampled by a scenario.** It is pure presentation, drawn from three tokens of the palette.
- **A live scenario that needs a light Mac** (S33, S55, S66, S68) is run with the Mac Light. If the Mac starts Dark, the run sets it Light for them under the leave of D10 and sets it back at the end.

- **Some terminal text cannot be read in some themes.** The findings table is the list. ANSI 15 of Solarized Light is its background, and ANSI 0 of every dark theme is near its background. This is the same in every terminal that uses these palettes, and is D5. The contrast note gives the number for the shown theme, and does not say which colours.
- **Catppuccin differs from Ghostty 1.3.1** (G2): Latte in ANSI 0 and 7 to 15, Mocha in ANSI 7 and 9 to 15. The values are those of the collection at the pinned commit.
- **A tuned mark is not the upstream accent.** `agent` of Catppuccin Latte is `148187`, not the teal `179299`: the upstream value has less than 3 on `bg2`. The terminal's ANSI 6 stays `179299`.
- **The fills of the two Solarized themes are mostly proposals.** Solarized has two background tones for each mode.
- **`agent` is not the brand accent** of GitHub or Catppuccin (G4).
- **Terminal colours 16 to 255, the scroll thumb, the shadows, the quit notice and the menus of the menu bar** are as 2610.0007 left them, in every theme.
- **The choice *Light* can give a dark app.** With a dark theme chosen for the light appearance, the tile *Light* puts a dark theme in effect, and the app's appearance is dark. The tile's title is the appearance the theme is chosen for; its picture shows the theme. The snapshot then has `"choice":"light"` and `"in_effect":"dark"`.
- **A menu of the menu bar can differ from the app.** It takes the Mac's appearance (2610.0007). With a dark theme on a light Mac it is light.
- **The Mac's appearance is read from a key that Apple does not document.** If a later macOS stops writing `AppleInterfaceStyle`, *Auto* reads Light at all times. S49 is the check, and it must be run again on a new macOS.
- **The automatic change of the Mac at sunset was not measured,** and neither were a launch on a dark Mac, a bundled app, and a tooltip under the app-level appearance (*Measured on 2026-10-06, for D10*). S49 changes the Mac by a script.
- **An HTML card follows light or dark, not the theme.** A document that follows gets the system's canvas (`1e1e1e` or `ffffff`) under every theme. Light or dark is the variant of the theme in effect, not the choice.
- **The host page of an HTML card has no live check after a change.** Its `--bg1` is not on screen, as in 2610.0007. The rule it uses is tested (S57); that `HTMLCardView` uses it is wiring.
- **The contrast note counts the terminal findings only.** A `chrome` finding is not counted (G14). No theme that ships has one, and #218 must decide this again for a theme of the user.
- **The showcase is a drawing.** It holds no real card and no terminal, and its text is not in the fonts of the Fonts pane.
- **The shown theme is not saved.** A window that opens shows the theme in effect.
- **Breeze is written for one appearance and not for the other.** `"theme_light":"breeze-dark"` is written and `"theme_light":"breeze-light"` is not, because only the second is the standard theme of that key.
- **The window is larger, for both panes.** The Fonts pane has empty room under its rows.
- **The list is not checked with more than eight rows.** That is #218.
- **"A ratio equal to the floor gives no finding" has no test.** The referee's change of `<` to `<=` left every test green. Its search found no grey pair, and no pair of the eight palettes, with a ratio of exactly 3, 4.5 or 7, so for 8-bit colours the two are most likely the same rule. This was not proved.
- **A theme has no name in another language.**
- **`Palette.variant` and `TerminalTheme.isDark` are the same rule written twice** (see *For the Implementing Agent*).
