## Context

The Settings window (#204, spec 2610.0005) has one row for each font role:
Terminal, Interface, Document. A row chooses a family and nothing else. The
sizes are fixed:

| Role | Size today | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Terminal | 16 pt | `Theme.termFontSize`, given to `TerminalView.init(fontSize:)` |
| Document | 14 px | `.doc-prose` in `DocTemplate.html`: `font-size: calc(14px * var(--oversample-k))` |
| Interface | 8 to 13 pt, by surface | literals at each `Theme.mono(…)` call |

What the code does today (`c611aa7`):

- `TerminalView.fontSize` is a `private let`, set at `init`. `fontFamily` is a
  settable property: a new value calls `rebuildFonts()`, which makes a new
  `TerminalRenderer`, adds one to `fontGeneration` and calls `relayout()`.
  `relayout()` calls `onResize` only when the columns, the rows or the cell
  changed.
- `tarmacDoc.fonts(chromeFamily, proseFamily)` in `doc-render.js` sets two
  custom properties and measures the prose again. `DocWebView.fontsChanged()`
  passes both values as `callAsyncJavaScript` arguments.
- The prose rules below `.doc-prose` are in `em` (code `0.8929em`, list
  indent `2.857em`), so they follow the prose size. The card's padding
  (18 px, 22 px) and its `max-width` (720 px) are in `px` and do not.
- `AppPrefs.Values` holds `warnBeforeQuit` and `fonts: [FontRole: String]`.
  `AppPrefs.encode` writes a literal; tests pin its bytes. `AppPrefsStore` is
  the one owner of the file.
- `FontSettings` resolves the family in effect for each role into
  `Theme.fontFamilies` and calls `onChange`; `AppController.fontsChanged()`
  broadcasts down every board's view tree (`FontFollowing`).
- `DevSnapshot.Fonts` reports, for each role, `saved` and a `face` or `css`.

Facts measured on 2026-10-06 (macOS 26, the dev machine), with the
`TerminalFonts.metrics` rule, cell in points as width × height:

| Size | system monospaced, 1× | system monospaced, 2× | Monaco, 1× | Monaco, 2× |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 13 | 8 × 16 | 8 × 15.5 | 8 × 18 | 8 × 17.5 |
| 13.5 | 8 × 16 | 8.5 × 16 | 8 × 19 | 8 × 18.5 |
| 16 | 10 × 19 | 10 × 19 | 10 × 22 | 9.5 × 21.5 |
| 16.5 | 10 × 20 | 10 × 19.5 | 10 × 23 | 10 × 22.5 |
| 20 | 12 × 24 | 12.5 × 24 | 12 × 27 | 12 × 27 |
| 32 | 20 × 38 | 20 × 38 | 19 × 43 | 19 × 43 |

- A half step can leave the cell as it is: the system face has the same cell
  at 13 and at 13.5 on a 1× display. The grid then does not change, and that
  is correct.
- 16 to 20 changes the cell's width and its height, for both faces, at both
  scales. Every scenario that must see the grid change uses 16 and 20.
- `JSONDecoder` reads `16`, `16.0` and `1.6e1` as the `Double` 16. It does
  not read `true`, `"16"`, `null` or `[16]` as a `Double`.
- `Double(String)` reads `"13.5"` and `"+16"`. It gives `nil` for `"13,5"`,
  `"16pt"`, `""` and `"abc"`, and a value that is not finite for `"nan"`,
  `"inf"` and `"1e400"`. It also reads `"1e1"` as 10 and `"0x10"` as 16.

AppKit facts, measured the same day in small probe programs that were not
the active app. They are strong indications, and the live run confirms them:

- An `NSTextField` sends its action on Return, and when the keyboard focus
  moves to another view (Tab). It does not send it when its window closes,
  and not when a stepper or a pop-up is pressed: the text stays in the
  field, not applied.
- A new `stringValue` set on a field that is being edited replaces the typed
  text, and no action is sent.
- Escape leaves the typed text in the field, still being edited.
- `NSStepper.valueWraps` is `true` by default: a step up at the top end gives
  the bottom end. With `valueWraps` false, the stepper still sends its
  action at an end, with the value it has.
- A held stepper repeats after 0.5 s, then each 0.1 s.
- A field that is made first responder has all its text selected, and so has
  it after Return. A click puts a caret in the text.
- Through the Accessibility API the field is an `AXTextField` and the
  stepper an `AXIncrementor`. `AXIncrement` and `AXDecrement` send the
  stepper's action. A new `AXValue` changes the field's text and sends
  nothing; `AXConfirm` then sends the action.

## Motivation

A family other than the default often needs another size to read well. The
board zoom is not a substitute: it scales every card, and it does not change
how many columns a terminal has (#206).

## Proposed Solution

### Overview

The Terminal row and the Document row of the Settings window each gain a
size: a number field and a stepper beside the family pop-up. The Interface
row gains nothing. A size changes in steps of 0.5. Terminal goes from 8 to 32
and starts at 16; Document goes from 10 to 24 and starts at 14.

A change applies at once to every open terminal card or markdown card on
every board, and is saved in `app-prefs.json`. The size and the family of a
role are independent: a change of one never changes the other.

### Key Components

- **`FontSizeRule`** (`app/Sources/TarmacKit/FontSizeRule.swift`, new) — the
  pure rules of one role's size: its range, its default, the step, which
  number the file may hold, which number a typed text becomes, and the text
  the field shows. `FontRole.sizeRule` gives a role's rule, or `nil` for
  Interface.
- **`AppPrefs.Values.fontSizes`** (`app/Sources/TarmacKit/AppPrefs.swift`) —
  the saved size of each role, beside `fonts`. Decode and encode gain the two
  keys.
- **`FontCSS.proseSize`** (`app/Sources/TarmacKit/FontCSS.swift`) — the CSS
  length a doc card is given.
- **`TerminalView.fontSize`** (`app/Sources/TarmacTerm/TerminalView.swift`) —
  becomes settable, by the path `fontFamily` uses.
- **`DocTemplate.html` and `doc-render.js`** — the prose size becomes the
  custom property `--prose-size`; `tarmacDoc.fonts` gains a third argument.
- **`DevSnapshot.Fonts`** (`app/Sources/TarmacKit/DevSnapshot.swift`) — a
  `size` for Terminal and for Document.
- **The shell** (`app/Sources/TarmacApp/`) — `SettingsWindowController` (the
  field and the stepper), `FontSettings` (the size in effect for each role,
  and the change), `Theme` (holds the sizes in effect; `termFontSize` goes),
  `FontFollowing` (a terminal takes the size again), `DocWebView` (hands the
  size to the page), `AppController+Terminals` (a new terminal starts on the
  size in effect), `Dev/DevSnapshotReader`.
- **The QA script** (`scripts/qa/settings-window.swift`) — verbs that read
  and set a row's size with no pointer.

### Data Flow

Launch:

1. `FontSettings` takes `AppPrefs.Values` from `AppPrefsStore`.
2. For Terminal and Document, `FontSizeRule.inEffect(saved:)` gives the size
   in effect: the saved one, or the role's default.
3. Each terminal card is created with the Terminal size in effect.
4. Each doc card, after its page loads, is given the Document size with the
   two families, in the one call it gets today.

A change in the window:

1. The stepper reports a number, or the field reports a text.
   `FontSizeRule.typed` turns a text into a size, or into `nil`.
2. For `nil`, and for a size equal to the one in effect, the field shows
   `FontSizeRule.text` of the size in effect again (a typed `13.50` becomes
   `13.5`). Nothing is saved and `onChange` is not called.
3. Otherwise `FontSettings` stores the size in the `Values`, saves the whole
   file through `AppPrefsStore`, sets the size in effect, and calls
   `onChange`. That is the broadcast a family change makes:
   - Terminal: each `TerminalView` takes `fontSize`. It rebuilds its
     renderer, lays its grid out again in the same card, and reports the new
     columns and rows, which go to the PTY as they do after a card resize.
     A terminal of a board that is not mounted takes the size at once, and
     its PTY is told the new grid when the board is mounted, as today
     (`TermGrid.resize`, `syncGrids(on:)`).
   - Document: each doc card is given the new prose size and measures again.
4. The field and the stepper of the row both show the size in effect.

A card made after a change starts on the sizes in effect. A change reaches
the cards of every board, mounted or not, and a culled card.

### Interface Contract

**The rule of a size.**

```swift
public struct FontSizeRule: Equatable, Sendable {
    public static let step = 0.5
    public let range: ClosedRange<Double>
    public let standard: Double                      // the size with nothing saved

    public func accepts(_ size: Double) -> Bool      // the file's rule
    public func inEffect(saved: Double?) -> Double   // saved when accepted, else standard
    public func nearest(to size: Double) -> Double   // the typed rule, for a finite number
    public func typed(_ text: String) -> Double?     // nil when the text is not a finite number
    public func choice(_ size: Double, saved: Double?) -> Double?   // nil when `size` is no change
    public static func text(_ size: Double) -> String
}

extension FontRole {
    public var sizeRule: FontSizeRule?   // nil for interface
    public var sizePrefsKey: String      // prefsKey + "_size"
}
```

| Role | `range` | `standard` | `sizePrefsKey` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `terminal` | 8…32 | 16 | `terminal_font_size` |
| `interface` | no rule | | `interface_font_size`, never read or written |
| `document` | 10…24 | 14 | `document_font_size` |

- `accepts` is true only for a number inside `range` (both ends included)
  that is a whole multiple of `step`.
- `nearest(to:)` first rounds to the nearest multiple of `step`, a tie going
  up (13.25 gives 13.5), then limits the result to `range`.
- `typed` drops the white space around the text and reads it with
  `Double(String)`. A text that does not read, or reads as a number that is
  not finite, gives `nil`. Any other gives `nearest(to:)` of the number. The
  decimal mark is `.` in every locale.
- `choice` gives `size` when the rule `accepts` it and it is not
  `inEffect(saved:)`, else `nil`. So a size already in effect is not saved,
  whatever the file holds.
- `text` is the shortest decimal form: `16`, `13.5`. Never `16.0`.
- The two rules differ on purpose. The file holds what the app wrote, so a
  number the app could not have written reads as the default. A typed number
  is the user's intent, so it goes to the nearest size the app has.

**The file.** `app-prefs.json` gains two keys:

| Key | Type | Absent means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `terminal_font_size` | number | 16 |
| `document_font_size` | number | 14 |

```swift
extension AppPrefs {
    public struct Values: Equatable, Sendable {
        public var warnBeforeQuit: Bool
        public var fonts: [FontRole: String]
        public var fontSizes: [FontRole: Double]     // no entry = the role's standard
        public init(warnBeforeQuit: Bool = true, fonts: [FontRole: String] = [:], fontSizes: [FontRole: Double] = [:])
    }
}
```

Decode rules, added to those of 2610.0005, which do not change:

- A size key is read on its own. It is an entry only when its value is a
  JSON number that the role's rule `accepts`. A boolean, a string, `null`,
  an array, an object, a number out of range and a number off the step are
  all no entry, and none changes how another key reads.
- A number that `StrictJSON` rejects is a different case, as it is today: a
  number that overflows a `Double` (`1e400`) damages the whole file, and
  every key reads as its default (S36).
- `16`, `16.0` and `1.6e1` are the same number and read as the entry 16.
- `interface_font_size` is an unknown key: ignored, and not written back.

Encode rules:

- The bytes of 2610.0005 first, unchanged: `{"warn_before_quit":<bool>`,
  then the family of each role that has one. Then one member for each role,
  in the order terminal, document, whose size entry the role's rule
  `accepts`. Then `}`. No white space.
- A size is written as a bare JSON number in the form of
  `FontSizeRule.text`: `"terminal_font_size":13.5`, `"document_font_size":18`.
- An entry the decode rule would refuse (out of range, off the step, not
  finite, or for Interface) is not written. So the bytes pass
  `StrictJSON.isValid` for every `Values`.
- With no size entry the bytes are exactly those of today.
- An entry equal to the role's standard is written like any other: it is a
  choice the user made.
- `decode(encode(v)) == v` for every `v` whose names and sizes pass the
  decode rules.

`Values.init` keeps its two arguments and gains a third with a default. So
`AppPrefsStrictnessTests` and every existing case of `AppPrefsTests` pass
unedited.

**Terminal.**

```swift
public final class TerminalView: NSView {
    public init(frame:, theme:, fontSize: CGFloat = 16, fontFamily: String? = nil, scrollbackLines:, backingScale:) throws
    public var fontSize: CGFloat    // a new value rebuilds the renderer and lays out; the same value does nothing
}
```

A rebuild for a new family or a new display scale keeps the size. A rebuild
for a new size keeps the family and the scale.

**CSS for doc cards.**

```swift
extension FontCSS {
    public static func proseSize(_ size: Double) -> String   // "14px", "13.5px"
}
```

`DocTemplate.html`:

```css
:root { --prose-size: 14px; }
.doc-prose { font-size: calc(var(--prose-size) * var(--oversample-k)); }
```

The template's default is the kit's: `FontCSS.proseSize` of the Document
rule's `standard`. `tarmacDoc.fonts(chromeFamily, proseFamily, proseSize)`
sets the three properties and measures again. `DocWebView` passes
`proseSize` as an argument, as it passes the families. No other rule of the
page changes. HTML cards are not given a size.

**The window.**

- The Terminal row and the Document row each have, on the right of the
  family pop-up, a number field and a stepper. The Interface row has only
  its pop-up. The three pop-ups have one width and one left edge.
- The field shows `FontSizeRule.text` of the size in effect. No unit is
  shown.
- The stepper moves by 0.5 and stops at the ends of the role's range: it
  does not wrap. Each step applies at once.
- A typed text applies when the field's editing ends: on Return, on Tab, and
  when the Settings window closes. AppKit does not end the editing on a
  close (see Context), so the window does. `typed` decides: a size applies
  and the field shows it; `nil` puts the field back to the size in effect.
  When editing has ended, the field shows a size that is in effect.
- A step is made from the size in effect. A press on the row's stepper while
  a typed text is not yet applied drops the text: with the size 16, `20`
  typed and one press up, the size is 16.5 and the field shows `16.5` (S35).
- A choice in a pop-up does not end the editing of a field. The typed text
  stays as it is until the editing ends.
- Escape is not handled: the typed text stays until the editing ends.
- The accessibility label of a field is `Terminal font size` or
  `Document font size`.
- The sample line of a row stays at the system font size. It shows the face,
  not the size.
- A size that cannot be saved still applies for the session; the failure is
  logged by `AppPrefsStore`, as for a family.
- Keys typed in the field are the field's. None reaches a card of the board
  (`takeKey` already ignores a key of another window).

**The dev snapshot.** Two of the `fonts` members gain `size`:

  "interface": {"saved": null,    "face": ".AppleSystemUIFontMonospaced-Regular"},

`size` is the size in effect, always a number. `interface` has no `size`

**No wire-protocol change.** Nothing here reaches `tarmacd` except the

### The shell, which is wiring

What stays in `TarmacApp` with no unit test, under exceptions 1 and 2 of

- `SettingsWindowController`: builds the field and the stepper from

- `FontSettings`: asks `FontSizeRule.inEffect` for each role that has a rule,

## Alternatives Considered

### A size for Interface

The chrome is set at many fixed sizes (8 to 13 pt), and card headers scale
them by the board zoom. One size does not fit them, and a scale factor
changes header layout and hit areas. Rejected by the user for this issue; a
scale factor is a separate decision.

### A pop-up of fixed sizes, or a slider

A pop-up cannot take a value the list does not have. A slider is hard to set
to an exact value. Rejected by the user in favour of a field and a stepper.

### Whole numbers only

Simpler, but a half point is the difference between two cells on a 2×
display (see the table in Context). Rejected by the user in favour of half
steps.

### Clamp an out-of-range value in the file

`64` would then read as 32. The issue asks that an out-of-range value reads
as the default, and that is the rule of every other key of the file: a value
the app could not have written is not used.

### Leave out a size equal to the default

The file would go back to its first bytes when the user steps back to 16. It
needs a second rule for when an entry is removed, and `decode(encode(v))`
would no longer be `v`. A size that is written is a choice; it costs nothing.

## Migration & Rollback

### Migration Steps

1. None. A file with no size key reads as 16 and 14, which are the sizes of
   today, so no card changes on the first launch after the upgrade.

### Rollback Plan

An older app ignores the two size keys when it reads the file, and drops
them the next time it saves it. No other data is touched.

## Security Considerations

A size is a number that reaches two sinks: a CSS length in doc cards and the
JSON file. It is never text the user controls: the field's text goes through
`FontSizeRule.typed`, which gives a finite number in a fixed range or
nothing. `FontCSS.proseSize` and `AppPrefs.encode` write that number in a
form made of digits and one `.`. `DocWebView` passes it as a script
argument, never as script text. The doc-card content policy and the
HTML-card sandbox do not change.

## For the Implementing Agent

> **Your job:** make every acceptance scenario above pass with tests that would *fail if the behavior were wrong*. A green suite that passes for the wrong reason does not satisfy this contract — `/verify` will hunt for vacuous tests by asking, of each behavior, "what is the smallest change that breaks this, and would any test catch it?"

Implement however you work best. Only the result is checked. The repository's
own rules still bind (`docs/coding-style.md`): red first and observed red;
the smallest correct diff; no line-noise comments; do not run `cargo fmt`;
verify with `make`, not the editor.

Points that are easy to get wrong here:

- **Two rules, not one.** The file refuses a number it could not have
  written (`accepts`). The field moves a typed number to the nearest size
  (`nearest`). If decode clamps, S24 fails; if the field refuses, S29 fails.
- **A boolean is not a number.** `JSONDecoder` does not read `true` as a
  `Double` (measured), but `JSONSerialization` bridges it to 1. Decode
  through the keyed container, as the family keys do. S23 is the check.
- **Encode writes a literal.** The size is `FontSizeRule.text`, not Swift's
  `description`: `16.0` in the file is wrong bytes (S4). An entry that
  `accepts` refuses is left out, not clamped (S25).
- **A half step may not move the grid.** The system face has one cell for
  13 and 13.5 at 1×. Use 16 and 20 wherever a test or a QA step must see the
  grid change, and assert first that the two cells differ.
- **The size and the family are two properties of one view.** A rebuild for
  either, or for a new display scale, must read both from the view (S27,
  S28). `fontsChanged()` sets both; a view whose two values are unchanged
  does nothing (S26).
- **One home for each default.** The number 16 for the terminal is in
  `FontSizeRule` only; `Theme.termFontSize` goes (S38). The number 14 is in
  `FontSizeRule` and once in the template, where S10 holds them equal.
  `TerminalView.init`'s own default of 16 stays: `TarmacTerm` does not
  depend on `TarmacKit`. So does the 16 in `TerminalFace.name(family:)`: a
  PostScript name does not depend on the size.
- **Comments and docs that say "two".** `AppPrefs.swift`, `FontCSS.swift`,
  `DevSnapshot.swift` and `DocTemplate.html` each have a comment that names
  the families alone. `make docs-check` fails on an ACTIVE doc that names a
  file in backticks before the file exists, and on a link to
  `qa/font-size-qa.md` before that file exists.
- **The page is given the size after it loads**, in the same call as the
  families. A doc card made after a change must not start at 14 (S19).
- **Cards that are not on screen.** The broadcast of 2610.0005 already
  reaches a culled card and the cards of a board that is not mounted. The
  size rides the same broadcast; do not add a second one.
- **The field must not rest on a value that is not in effect.** After a
  refused text, and after a clamped one, the field shows the size in effect
  (S29). The stepper and the field show one number.
- **AppKit does not finish the field for you.** See the AppKit facts in
  Context. The window must end the field's editing when it closes, or a
  typed text is lost and still shows when the window opens again (S35). A
  stepper press must leave the field on the size in effect (S35).
- **A family change must not write the size field.** A new `stringValue`
  drops a text that is being typed. The pop-up's handler leaves the row's
  size field as it is.
- **`NSStepper` wraps by default.** Set `valueWraps` to false, or a step up
  at 32 gives 8. The stepper still sends its action at an end, so the rule
  "a size equal to the one in effect is not a change" is what keeps the
  file as it is (S29, S34).
- **Compare with the size in effect, not with the saved entry.**
  `FontSettings.choose` compares a family with the saved one. A size copied
  from it writes `16` over a file that holds a refused `64` (S30, S41).
- **Each row has its own rule.** A Document field built from the Terminal
  rule passes every scenario that uses 18. S34 is the check.
- **One owner of the file.** A size is stored through `AppPrefsStore`, like
  a family. S8 is the kit check; S18 is the live one.
- **Never launch `dist/Tarmac.app`** and never kill a daemon by name. All
  live QA is against `make run`; stop its daemon with `make kill-daemon`.
  Run `make qa` in an app that has not shown the Settings window.

Test principles, the ones `/verify` scores against:

- **Behavioral over structural** — assert observable output, not internals.
- **Every test can fail** — no copied expected value, no tautology.
- **Deterministic, isolated, readable** — temp directories per test, no
  shared state, no dependence on a font beyond Monaco and the system faces.

## Trade-offs and Limitations

- **Interface has no size.** The chrome sizes stay fixed. A scale factor is
  a separate issue.
- **A half step can do nothing visible in a terminal.** The cell is rounded
  to whole device pixels, so two sizes can share a cell.
- **The card does not follow the size.** A terminal card keeps its width and
  height and holds fewer or more cells. A doc card keeps its width, its
  padding and its 720 px limit, and wraps sooner at a larger size.
- **A size below the default is small at 100% zoom.** 8 pt is the low end on
  purpose; the board zoom still applies on top.
- **The decimal mark is `.`** in the field, in every locale. `13,5` is
  refused and the field goes back.
- **The field takes what the input source gives.** Under an input source
  such as Zhuyin a digit key gives a symbol that is no number, and the field
  goes back. The stepper needs no typing.
- **Each step saves the file and resizes every terminal.** A held stepper
  does this ten times a second. Each resize is one message to the daemon for
  each terminal of the mounted board whose grid changed; a terminal of
  another board sends its message when that board is mounted.
- **A text typed and not applied is lost when the app quits** with the
  Settings window open. The window applies it on a close only.
- **Escape does not cancel a typed text**, and a choice in a pop-up does not
  apply it. The text is applied, or dropped, when the editing ends.
- **The field reads what `Double(String)` reads.** `1e1` is 10 and `0x10` is
  16. No scenario pins these forms.
- **A size the user stepped back to the default stays in the file**, as
  `"terminal_font_size":16`.
- **The sample line does not show the size.** At 32 pt it would not fit the
  row.
- **HTML cards are not affected**, as for the families.
- **`size` in the snapshot reports what the app resolved**, not what each
  view drew. `term.cols`, `term.rows` and `scroll.total` are the witnesses.
- **An older app drops the size keys** when it next saves the file.
