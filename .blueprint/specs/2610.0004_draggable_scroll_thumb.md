## Context

Every card draws one scroll thumb from a `ScrollMetrics {offset, visible,
total}` its content reports (`ScrollIndicator`, spec 2610.0003). The thumb is
look-only: `ScrollThumbView.hitTest` answers nil, `CardView.hitTest` hands
every point in a resize zone to the grip, and the wheel router gives a card
the wheel only for a view inside the body's container. It shows when the
selected card takes a wheel and fades a second after
(`ScrollIndicator.Visibility`); the shell arms a timer from the two durations.

Nothing scrolls a content *to* a position. A terminal scrolls by rows or to
its ends (`ViewportScroll`); a markdown page and an HTML document scroll only
by a wheel that reaches them. The host tells an HTML document two things,
`zoom` and `cull` (`CardHostMessage`).

## Motivation

WebKit's scrollbars are hidden, so nothing on a card can be dragged to
scroll: a terminal with 1860 rows is 123 screens of wheel, end to end. The
thumb is 10 wide and looks like something to grab; on seeing it, the user
asked to move it with the pointer (#193).

## Proposed Solution

### Overview

A press on the thumb while it shows drags it. A pure value keeps how far
below the thumb's top the press landed and turns each pointer position into
an offset in the content's own unit; the card hands that offset to its
content, which scrolls there and reports where it is; the thumb is drawn from
the report, as for a wheel. While the pointer is over the thumb or holds it,
the thumb does not fade. Where the thumb shows it takes the press ahead of
the resize strip; a faded thumb takes nothing.

### Key Components

- **`ScrollIndicator.Visibility`** (`TarmacKit`, `ScrollIndicatorTests`) —
  gains the pointer and the press as things that hold the thumb, says whether
  it can be grabbed, and says when the fade starts.
- **`ScrollDrag`** (new, `app/Sources/TarmacKit/ScrollDrag.swift`, with
  `ScrollDragTests`) — the grab and the inverse of `ScrollIndicator.thumb`.
- **`CardHit`** (new, `app/Sources/TarmacKit/CardHit.swift`, with
  `CardHitTests`) — what a point on a card is: the thumb, a resize handle or
  the content.
- **`BoardWheel.route`** — is told the pointer is over the thumb.
- **`CardHostMessage.scrollTo`** and the shim's `scrollTo` branch — the host
  scrolls an HTML document's root to a position.
- **`ViewportScroll.row`** and **`TerminalView.scroll(to:)`** (`TarmacTerm`)
  — a terminal's viewport to a row.
- **`tarmacDoc.scrollTo`** (`doc-render.js`) and **`DocCardBody.scroll(to:)`**
  — the two web bodies.
- **`DevSnapshot.Scroll.thumb`** — the thumb's rect, for a tool outside the
  app.
- **`scripts/qa/wheel-gesture.swift`** — learns to move, press, drag in
  steps, sample and release.
- **The shell**: `CardView.hitTest`, `ScrollThumbView`'s mouse and wheel
  handlers and cursor, `CardView+Scroll`'s drag and timer,
  `HoverCursorRouter`'s word on the thumb, `AppController.routeScroll` and
  the terminal's scroll-to callback.

### Data Flow

1. A wheel on the selected card shows the thumb (2610.0003). The card then
   asks whether the pointer is over the thumb.
2. `CardView.hitTest` asks `CardHit.at` with the point, the thumb's frame,
   `Visibility.grabbable(atMs:)` and the handle under the point, and returns
   the thumb view, the grip, or what AppKit found.
3. `HoverCursorRouter`, on each pointer move, hit-tests the window as it does
   today, and the selected card is told whether the hit is its thumb:
   `pointer(over: true)` when it is, `pointer(over: false)` when it is not.
4. A `mouseDown` on the thumb: `Visibility.pressed(atMs:)`, and the card
   keeps a `ScrollDrag(pointerY:thumb:)` and starts to watch the
   application's events for the left button coming up.
5. Each `mouseDragged`: `drag.offset(pointerY:metrics:body:covered:scale:)`
   with the card's metrics and layout *then*. A non-nil answer goes to the
   content: `docBody.scroll(to:)`, or for a terminal the card's
   `onScrollTo` callback, which `AppController` wires to
   `TerminalView.scroll(to:)`.
6. The content scrolls and reports as it does for a wheel
   (`onScrollChanged`); the card lays the thumb out from the report, then
   asks whether the pointer is over it.
7. The release — read off the application's events, not the thumb's own:
   the drag is dropped, `Visibility.released(atMs:)`, and the pointer is
   asked once more.
8. After any change of the `Visibility` the card applies it: the view at 1
   while `alpha(atMs:)` is above 0 and at 0 otherwise, and its one timer
   armed for `fadeStartsAtMs`.

### Interface Contract

```swift
// TarmacKit — ScrollIndicator.Visibility, added to what it has
public mutating func pointer(over: Bool, atMs now: UInt64)
public mutating func pressed(atMs now: UInt64)
public mutating func released(atMs now: UInt64)
public func grabbable(atMs now: UInt64) -> Bool
public func fadeStartsAtMs(atMs now: UInt64) -> UInt64?
```

**`Visibility`** holds three things: when the hold last started (a wheel, or
the last holder letting go), whether the pointer is over the thumb, and
whether a press holds it.

- `alpha(atMs:)` is 1 while the pointer is over or a press holds; otherwise
  it is what it is today, counted from when the hold last started; 0 when it
  never did.
- `pointer(over: true, atMs:)` is kept only when `alpha(atMs:)` is above 0 at
  that moment: a pointer over a thumb that is not showing is not over it.
  Told again while over, it changes nothing.
- `pointer(over: false, atMs:)` while over: the pointer is off, and unless a
  press holds, the hold starts again at `now`. Told while not over, it
  changes nothing — it does not move the hold.
- `pressed(atMs:)`: a press holds. It is taken whatever the alpha: the hit
  rule is the gate.
- `released(atMs:)` while pressed: the press is over, and unless the pointer
  is over, the hold starts again at `now`. With no press held it changes
  nothing.
- `wheeled(atMs:)`: as today, the hold starts at `now`.
- `reset()`: all three are dropped; the value equals a new `Visibility()`.
- `grabbable(atMs:)` is `alpha(atMs:) > 0`.
- `fadeStartsAtMs(atMs:)` is the instant the hold ends — its start plus
  `holdMs` — while `alpha(atMs:)` is above 0 and nothing holds the thumb:
  inside the fade too, where that instant is already past. It is nil while
  the pointer is over or a press holds, and nil when `alpha(atMs:)` is 0. The
  sum saturates at `UInt64.max`: as before, no time traps.

```swift
// TarmacKit — ScrollDrag.swift
public struct ScrollDrag: Equatable, Sendable {
    /// How far below the thumb's top the press landed, in screen points.
    public let grab: CGFloat
    /// `thumb` is the thumb's frame as drawn at the press — what
    /// `ScrollIndicator.frame` last answered — and `pointerY` is in the
    /// coordinates that frame is in: `grab = pointerY − thumb.minY`.
    public init(pointerY: CGFloat, thumb: CGRect)
    /// The offset that puts the thumb's top `grab` above the pointer, on the
    /// track as `ScrollIndicator.frame` lays it out from these arguments; nil
    /// when there is no thumb, or no room for it to move.
    public func offset(
        pointerY: CGFloat, metrics: ScrollMetrics?, body: CGRect, covered: CGFloat, scale: CardScale
    ) -> Double?
}
```

`offset` is the inverse of `ScrollIndicator.thumb`. Its `body`, `covered` and
`scale` are what `ScrollIndicator.frame` takes — `body` is
`CardBox.Screen.shownBody` — and `pointerY` is in the coordinates `body` is
given in, the thumb's frame being in the same:

```
track  = body.height / scale.zoom − inset − max(CardBox.cornerRadius, covered + inset)   // as `frame` has it
t      = ScrollIndicator.thumb(metrics, track: track)            // nil → nil
free   = track − t.length                                        // free <= 0 → nil
y      = (pointerY − grab − body.minY) / scale.zoom − inset      // the thumb's top along the track, world units
offset = clamp(y, 0 … free) / free × (total − visible)
```

`offset` reads no drawn frame and snaps nothing, whatever `scale.backing` is:
the content snaps for itself. Only `grab` comes off a drawn frame, whose `y`
is on a whole device pixel, so the thumb's top can sit up to half a device
pixel from the pointer less the grab. S26's tolerance covers it.

```swift
// TarmacKit — CardHit.swift
public enum CardHit: Equatable, Sendable {
    case thumb
    case handle(CardResize.Handle)
    case content
    /// `point` and `thumb` in one coordinate space; `thumb` nil when none is laid out.
    public static func at(
        _ point: CGPoint, thumb: CGRect?, grabbable: Bool, handle: CardResize.Handle?
    ) -> CardHit
}
```

`.thumb` when `grabbable`, `thumb` is not nil and holds the point (its
minimum edges inside, its maximum edges outside, as a handle's zone);
otherwise `.handle` when `handle` is not nil; otherwise `.content`.

```swift
// TarmacKit — BoardWheel
public static func route<ID: Equatable>(
    pinch: Bool, over card: ID?, inBody: Bool, onThumb: Bool, selected: ID?
) -> Route
```

`.card` for the selected card when `inBody` or `onThumb`; everything else as
today.

```swift
// TarmacKit — CardHostMessage
case scrollTo(Double)        // {"tarmac":"scrollTo","y":<number>}

// TarmacTerm
public enum ViewportScroll { case top, bottom, rows(Int), row(Int) }
extension TerminalView { public func scroll(to offset: Double) }

// TarmacKit — DevSnapshot.Scroll
public var thumb: CGRect?    // in the coordinates of the card's `screenRect`; nil when none is laid out
public init(metrics: ScrollMetrics, laidOut: Bool, alpha: CGFloat, thumb: CGRect?)

// TarmacApp — DocCardBody
//   func scroll(to offset: Double)      // in the unit of the metrics the body reports
```

- **`ViewportScroll.row(n)`** is libghostty's absolute-row scroll
  (`GHOSTTY_SCROLL_VIEWPORT_ROW`), `n` below 0 sent as 0.
- **`TerminalView.scroll(to:)`** ignores an offset that is not finite; else
  rounds it to the nearest row (a tie away from zero), scrolls the engine
  there, and schedules the frame read a wheel schedules, so the view redraws
  and `onScrollChanged` is told. An offset past what a row number holds is
  the nearest end, and does not trap.
- **The shim** (`card_shim.js`), on a message from its parent whose `tarmac`
  is `"scrollTo"` and whose `y` is a finite number, calls
  `window.scrollTo({left: window.scrollX, top: y, behavior: "instant"})`. Any
  other `y`, and the same message from any other source, does nothing and
  throws nothing. It asks for no native frame and sets no timer for it,
  paused or not. It still knows no message named `scroll`.
- **`tarmacDoc.scrollTo(y)`** sets the scroller's `scrollTop` for a finite
  `y`.
- **Snapshot** — a card's `scroll` object gains `"thumb"`:
  `{"x", "y", "w", "h"}`, or `null` when no thumb is laid out or the card is
  culled. `DevSnapshot` decides it, as it decides `shown`: `null` when
  `laidOut` is false, whatever rect was handed over, and when none was.
  `shown` keeps its rule, `laidOut && alpha > 0`, with the alpha now 1 while
  the thumb is held.

**Breaking, inside the app only:** `BoardWheel.route` and
`DevSnapshot.Scroll.init` each gain a parameter; `DocCardBody` gains a
requirement; `CardHostMessage` gains a case (its exhaustive test is
rewritten on purpose); a snapshot key is added.

### The shell, which is wiring

- **`CardView.hitTest`**: `CardHit.at` with the point and the thumb's frame
  in one space, `scrollVisibility.grabbable(atMs: Uptime.nowMs)` and
  `resizeHandle(at:)`; returns `scrollThumb`, `grip`, or `super`'s hit.
  `ScrollThumbView.hitTest` keeps answering nil.
- **`ScrollThumbView`**: `mouseDown`, `mouseDragged` and `scrollWheel` go to
  closures the card sets, as `CardResizeGrip`'s do; its `mouseUp` does
  nothing, and it does not accept first responder. It is a
  `HoverCursorProviding`: the arrow, and it claims pointer moves over a doc
  card, as the grip does.
- **`CardView+Scroll`**: holds `scrollDrag: ScrollDrag?`. The press makes it,
  each drag event asks it and hands a non-nil offset on, the release drops
  it. A deselect (`hideScrollThumb`) and `cancelGesture()` drop it too, and
  nothing else does but a fresh press. While no thumb is laid out its view
  is hidden, and AppKit sends a hidden view no drag event (the first run's
  finding): the drag stays held, and goes on from the pointer once a thumb
  is laid out again. It sends it no `mouseUp` either, so the release is not
  the view's to hear: from the press until the release the card watches the
  application's events for the left button coming up (a local monitor), and
  that is what ends the drag, whether the thumb is laid out or not. The
  watch ends with the drag, however the drag ends.
  Pointer positions are read in the clip's coordinates, the thumb's
  superview. `applyScrollVisibility()`, run whenever the `Visibility` value
  changed, stops the timer and any fade, puts the view at 1 when
  `alpha(atMs:)` is above 0 — at 0 otherwise — and arms the timer for
  `fadeStartsAtMs` when that is not nil, at once for an instant already
  past; the timer runs the `fadeMs` animation. `pointerIsOverThumb` is the
  window's hit test at the pointer's position answering this card's thumb;
  it is asked after each thumb layout, after a wheel is recorded and at the
  release, and told to the `Visibility`.
- **`HoverCursorRouter`**: on each move it says whether the hit is a thumb,
  and the board's selected card — the one card whose thumb can show — is
  told `pointer(over:)` with whether that thumb is its own. It keeps no
  memory of where the pointer was: a card that learned of the pointer by
  asking, after a wheel, a layout or a release, is told when it leaves.
  `pointerLeft()` — the pointer left the board — tells the selected card
  false.
- **`AppController.routeScroll`**: passes `onThumb`, the hit being the
  card's thumb. **The thumb's wheel** goes to the view the body's container
  finds at that point (`bodyHost.hitTest`), as the HTML shield hands its
  wheel on.
- **Scroll-to**: `DocWebView.scroll(to:)` runs `tarmacDoc.scrollTo`;
  `HTMLCardView.scroll(to:)` posts `.scrollTo`, as it posts a zoom, once its
  page is loaded; a terminal card's `onScrollTo` is set where its metrics
  callback is, and calls its session's `view.scroll(to:)`.
- **`DevSnapshotReader`**: hands over the thumb view's frame in the
  coordinates `screen_rect` is read in, beside `laidOut` as it reads it
  today. It decides nothing: a hidden thumb's stale frame is `DevSnapshot`'s
  to drop.

## Alternatives Considered

Argued in the design doc: the thumb drawn at the pointer while held (the
fallback, should S26 fail); WebKit's scrollbar back for the web kinds;
moving the content by posted wheels; the thumb answering its own hit test;
showing on hover or always; a thumb clear of the resize strip.

## Security Considerations

`scrollTo` is a third message from the host to a card's sandboxed document.
The shim takes it only from `window.parent`, as it does `zoom` and `cull`, so
a document, or a frame inside it, cannot send it; and it takes only a finite
number, which it hands to `window.scrollTo`. It gives the host nothing a
wheel does not already do, and the document can still cancel or undo the
scroll. Nothing new crosses from the document to the app.

## Acceptance Scenarios

`[Q]` scenarios run against the live dev app with
`scripts/qa/wheel-gesture.swift` and `tarmac dev snapshot`, and are recorded
in `qa/scroll-thumb-drag-qa.md` with the values observed and, where one is
named, the one-line knockout's. The setup is 2610.0003's: its probe, its two
markdown fixtures, its way of selecting a card.

**The cards.** A card is 392 × 310 at zoom 1 unless said otherwise. The
terminal is resized to that (`tarmac dev resize <term> 392x310`) and given
300 lines of history (`seq 1 300`). So at zoom 1 the thumb is 24 long on all
three kinds, its middle 12 below its top, and it lies at card x 379–389,
where the right resize strip is x 386–392 from y 20 to y 290 and the
bottom-right corner zone x 372–392 from y 290. A place on the card is
`scroll.thumb` less the card's `screen_rect` origin; the script's `--at` and
`--then <x>,<y>` are from the body's top-left, 1 across and 31 down from the
card's at zoom 1.

**`free`** is the thumb's free travel on screen, `(track − length) × zoom`,
read off the snapshot as the body's height (`screen_rect.h − 32 × zoom`) less
`12 × zoom`, less `scroll.thumb.h`: 242 on these cards at zoom 1.

**A wheel that moves nothing** is one notch toward the edge the content is
at: `--lines --dy 1 --events 1` at the top, `--dy -1` at the end. With no
phases it cannot rubber-band, so no report and no layout follows it
(2610.0003 S36). **Any wheel** is the script's default gesture; it shows the
thumb and may move the content.

**The pointer between calls.** Without `--stay` the script puts the pointer
back where it found it. So a call that follows one which left the pointer on
a thumb puts it back on that thumb, and the thumb does not fade. Wherever a
scenario waits for `shown` to read false, the pointer is first moved off and
left: `--events 0 --at center --stay`.

- **S15 [Q] each kind follows the thumb.** On the long markdown fixture, the

  the terminal at its live bottom, the two web cards after one gesture of

  (`--then thumb --press --drag 0,<n> --release`). (1) After any wheel,
  `2 × free` up: then `scroll.offset` is 0. (2) After a wheel that moves
  nothing, `free / 2` down: then `offset` is half of `total − visible`, to
  the larger of 1 % of it or one unit. (3) After any wheel, `free` down: then
  `offset + visible = total` (± 1 for the two web kinds).

  Twenty lines are written to its tty from another shell

  typing in the terminal brings the viewport down by itself, and
  `term.scrollback_tail` ends at the cursor wherever the viewport is). After
  (2) they grow `total` and leave `offset` where it was; after (3) they grow
  `total` and leave `offset + visible = total`.

  after a wheel that moves nothing, a press and a release on the thumb with
  no drag leave `offset` 0, and the same with `--drag 0,1 --steps 1` makes it
  198 ± 2 — `(total − visible) / free`.
  *Knockouts*, one line each: the card hands a terminal's offset to no one →
  the terminal's `offset` does not change; `tarmacDoc.scrollTo` sets nothing

## For the Implementing Agent

> **Your job:** make every acceptance scenario above pass with tests that
> would *fail if the behavior were wrong*. `/verify` hunts for vacuous tests
> by asking, of each behavior, "what is the smallest change that breaks
> this, and would any test catch it?"

- **Test-first** (`docs/coding-style.md` §1): each unit scenario's test is
  watched failing before the code that passes it. Decisions live in
  `TarmacKit` and `TarmacTerm`; `TarmacApp` reads state, calls them and
  applies the answer.
- **Order.** Kit and engines first (S1–S14, S20–S25, S31–S35), then the
  gesture script, then the wiring. **Run S26 before the other live
  scenarios**: it decides whether the thumb is drawn from reports at all.
- **The gesture script** (`scripts/qa/wheel-gesture.swift`) gains, beside
  what it has: `--events 0` (no wheel, where it refused a count under 1);

  snapshot and move the pointer to its `scroll.thumb` — the middle, or that
  many points from its top-left — or to a point of the body); `--press` (at
  the pointer, click state 1); `--drag <dx>,<dy>` with `--steps <n>`
  (default 10) and `--step-ms <ms>` (default 16, the wait after each step);
  `--sample <ms>` (after each step and its `--step-ms`, wait that much
  more, read the snapshot, and print the pointer's y in the snapshot's
  coordinates, the grab, the thumb's y, `offset` and the read's duration)
  and `--sample-last <ms>` (the same, once, after the last step and its

  put the pointer back); `--key escape` (an Escape key-down and key-up at
  the HID tap, which the key window takes, after any press and before any

  is, and notes the press in a file beside the dev socket. The next call

  on from the pointer, with `--key`, `--drag` or `--release` alone, and its
  `--release` ends the press. A note with no left button down — a call cut
  short — is dropped, and that call runs as one with no press held. Its
  other refusals stand, and it gains four: S36's three, and `--press` over
  a press already held.

## Trade-offs and Limitations

From the design: the thumb trails the pointer by the content's report, and a
terminal's moves a row at a time; where the thumb shows, the edge under it
does not resize; a host-to-document scroll message is back; a program
tracking the mouse, a borrowed document and markdown text miss presses under
the thumb; a thumb under a resting pointer stays; only an HTML document's
root is dragged.

- The cursor over the thumb is read off a capture or by eye (S37), on a
  markdown card and a terminal: the snapshot does not say which cursor
  shows. Over an HTML card's thumb it is not read.
- Two paths have no scenario and are reviewed, not run. The pointer leaving
  the board from a thumb (`HoverCursorRouter.pointerLeft()`): the script aims
  nowhere but the dev window. A card removed, or leaving its window, under a
  held thumb (`cancelGesture()`): no verb removes a card or switches a
  board.
- A release with no thumb is run for content that now fits (S29). A
  reload's nil, a cull and the alternate screen hide the thumb the same way
  and are not run.
- A board pan or zoom under a still pointer is not one of the moments the
  pointer is asked; the answer corrects at the next move.
- A release taken by a tracking loop of its own (a menu's, say) is not one
  the card's watch sees: the press then lasts until the next release of the
  left button anywhere in the app, which ends it.
- A thumb shown by a wheel in a window that is not key, under a resting
  pointer, holds until the window is key and the pointer moves.
- `tarmacDoc.scrollTo` has no unit test: nothing runs `doc-render.js`
  outside the app. S15's markdown case and its knockout are its check.
- `behavior: "instant"` is the CSSOM's word. S11 pins that the shim asks for
  it; that it beats a document's `scroll-behavior: smooth` is not measured
  (the probe sets none), and where it fails only that document's thumb
  trails.
- `[Q]` scenarios are not in `make qa`.

## Open Questions

- **Thumb latency** was not known, and S26 measures it. Its bar, 50 ms on
  every kind, is the user's (design, *Confirmed*). S26's record in the QA
  sheet is the measurement: the thumb stays drawn from reports, and no
  fallback was written. A snapshot takes 71 to 86 ms to read, so S26 alone
  says "within 50 to 136 ms"; the sheet also records the lag timed inside
  the app by a probe that is not shipped — 5 to 17 ms from a drag event to
  the layout that moved the thumb, on each kind, in the stepped drag. S26
  by itself does not resolve the 50 ms; that figure does.
- **A document that scrolls itself during a drag** (a `scroll` handler that
  snaps back) is not measured: the thumb follows what it reports.
- **Three things the live scenarios leant on were read off the code, not
  measured**, and the first run settled each (2026-10-05). S29's second
  half: a thumb view hidden under a held press is sent *no* drag event —
  three posted, none received — and is sent them again once it is laid out;
  the drag stays held all the same, which is what S29 checks, and its
  knockout was replaced by one that the scenario can reach.
  S15: lines written to a terminal's tty from another shell do arrive as its
  output. S28: the terminal does *not* keep the keys through a click on the
  probe's shield — they go to the board — so the driver's `key` verb cannot
  type the Escape; Escape typed at the app by the script deselects the probe
  (`EscLadder`'s `deselectDoc`) and leaves the held press with the thumb.
  S28 says so now, and the script gained `--key escape`; what S28 checks is
  as it was. S37 lost a control the same way: a markdown card shows the
  arrow over its text too, so it offers no I-beam to tell the thumb's arrow
  from, and the terminal carries that contrast alone.
